# Estimating the Horizon of Articles to Decide When to Stop Searching in Systematic Reviews (osteoporosis clinical decision support tools)

## Source structure and scope
Kastner M, Straus S, Goldsmith CH. AMIA Annual Symposium Proceedings 2007: 389-393 (PMID
18693864, PMC2655834). Retrospective application of capture-mark-recapture (CMR) to one
systematic review's searches. Engagement depth: full article page via fetch tool; the specific
sentences below were returned as quotations.

## Anchored details
- **Method:** four databases (MEDLINE, EMBASE, CINAHL, EBM Reviews) treated as capture
  occasions; each of 1,246 potentially relevant articles coded for presence in each database;
  Poisson regression (log-linear) models fitted in Egret with an iterative proportional fitting
  algorithm to estimate the missing cell and a 95% CI.
- **Assumption statements:** "the basic principle behind the CMR concept is that there are k
  independent samples of database searches"; closed population assumed.
- **Results:** "The horizon was estimated to be 1838 articles (95% CI 1749 to 1955)", i.e. an
  estimated 592 articles missing; "the systematic review captured 68% of known articles from 4
  large bibliographic databases."
- **Stopping-rule idea:** "the decision to either continue searching subsequent databases or to
  stop if the known number of articles falls within the confidence intervals of the horizon
  estimate"; the optimal search order would have been MEDLINE, EMBASE, EBM reviews, CINAHL.
- **Caution:** the result "has to be interpreted with caution for several reasons"; tested
  retrospectively; "more studies are needed to objectively define cutpoints for these
  estimates"; uncertainty whether missing articles are excluded studies, unpublished work, or
  search-design shortfalls.
