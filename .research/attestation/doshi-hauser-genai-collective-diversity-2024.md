# Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content

## Source structure and scope
Anil R. Doshi and Oliver P. Hauser; Science Advances, published 2024-07-12 (PMC open-access full
text). Pre-registered online experiment. Read via PMC full text (methods, results, discussion,
limitations).

## Anchored details
- **Design:** 293 writers (from 500 recruited) wrote eight-sentence short stories in three conditions —
  human-only; optional one GPT-4 three-sentence story idea; up to five GPT-4 ideas. 600 separate
  evaluators gave 3,519 evaluations. "88.4% of participants chose to call upon generative AI at least once".
- **Measures:** Novelty index (novel, original, rare; alpha 0.92); usefulness index (alpha 0.89); story
  similarity = cosine similarity of OpenAI-embedding vectors (x100) to the average of other stories in
  the same condition and to the AI ideas used.
- **Individual effect:** five GenAI ideas raised novelty by 8.1% (b = 0.311, P < 0.001) and usefulness by
  9.0% (b = 0.453, P < 0.001) versus human-only.
- **Collective effect:** similarity increase from one or five AI ideas "represents 10.7% and 8.9% of the
  total range, respectively"; AI-condition stories were 5.2% and 5.0% more similar to the AI ideas.
- **Heterogeneity:** gains concentrated among low-creativity writers (Divergent Association Task);
  "Among the most inherently creative writers ... there is little effect".
- **Interpretation:** "With generative AI, writers are individually better off, but collectively a
  narrower scope of novel content is produced" — framed as a social dilemma.
- **Limitations:** constrained task (eight sentences, short story); "there is no interactiveness with the
  LLM or variation in prompts"; non-professional Prolific sample; may not generalize to less-constrained tasks.
