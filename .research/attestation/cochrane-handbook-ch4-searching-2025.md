# Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions, Chapter 4: Searching for and selecting studies

## Source structure and scope
Lefebvre C, Glanville J, Briscoe S, Featherstone R, Littlewood A, Metzendorf M-I, Noel-Storr A,
Paynter R, Rader T, Thomas J, Wieland LS, on behalf of the Cochrane Information Retrieval
Methods Group. "Last updated March 2025", in Cochrane Handbook version 6.5.1 (as printed in
the chapter's "Cite this chapter as"). Methodological guidance. Engagement depth: full chapter
text retrieved; section 4.4.11 "When to stop searching" and MECIR boxes 4.3.a and 4.3.e read
closely.

## Anchored details
- **MECIR C19 (Mandatory):** "Design searches to capture as many studies as possible that meet
  the eligibility criteria".
- **MECIR Box 4.3.e:** grey literature (C28) and searching within previous reviews (C29) rated
  "Highly desirable"; authors "should ... check reference lists of included studies and
  relevant systematic reviews".
- **4.4.11, para 1:** "It is often difficult to decide in a scientific or objective way when a
  search is complete"; the ability to decide "typically develops through experience". Proposed
  rules include stopping "if adding in a series of new terms ... yields no new relevant records,
  or if precision falls below a particular cut-off point". "In topics where evidence is scarce,
  authors might need to be more cautious". "There has been little formal evaluation of the
  approaches".
- **4.4.11, para 2 (known-item check and its pitfall):** "One simple test is to check whether the
  search is finding the publications that have been recommended as key publications or that
  have been included in other similar reviews." But "It is not enough ... for the strategy to
  find only those records, otherwise this might be a sign that the strategy is biased towards
  known studies". If citation searches and reference checking find documents the searches had
  already retrieved "but that the team did not necessarily know about in advance, then this is
  one sign that the strategy might be performing adequately." PRESS should be used; missing
  PRESS dimensions mean "the search may not yet be complete".
- **4.4.11, para 3:** capture-recapture and relative recall named as statistical techniques;
  Kastner's approach would "entail potentially an iterative search and selection process";
  "Capture-recapture needs results from at least two searches"; "Further investigation of
  published prospective techniques seems warranted".
- **4.4.11, para 4:** relative recall "requires a range of searches to have been conducted" and
  "may be most useful at the end of the search process".
- **4.4.11, para 5:** "If the citation searches yield a large number of additional relevant
  records, this may suggest that the original searches were not optimal and should be revisited."
- **4.4.11, paras 6-7:** for qualitative evidence, searching "stops when new information ceases
  to be identified" and justifications "may centre around saturation" (citing Booth 2016); for
  complex topics, "the issue of knowing when to stop may take on a different focus".
