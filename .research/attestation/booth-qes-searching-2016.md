# Searching for qualitative research for inclusion in systematic reviews: a structured methodological review

## Source structure and scope
Booth A. Systematic Reviews 2016; 5: 74 (DOI 10.1186/s13643-016-0249-x, PMC4855695).
Structured methodological review built from 15 "citation pearls" and their citing references
(1063 references including duplicates). Engagement depth: full text; sampling, saturation,
"How many studies are enough?" and "When can I stop searching?" sections read closely.

## Anchored details
- **Two schools:** purposive sampling "until data saturation is reached" versus retrieving "all
  relevant studies in a field rather than a sample of them".
- **Diversity vs more-of-the-same:** "Adding additional electronic databases to a search
  protocol (i.e. to search for more of the same) runs counter to the strategy of seeking to
  diversify a sample (i.e. purposively to move on to different, more productive lines of
  inquiry)." "Several authors comment on the value of identifying the disconfirming case, and
  search strategies may be targeted specifically to achieve such insights."
- **Borrowed techniques:** "deliberately seeking studies to act as negative cases, aiming for
  maximum variability and designing results set to be heterogeneous".
- **Table 3:** maps synthesis methods to sampling methods, e.g. meta-interpretation uses
  "Maximal divergent sampling/maximum variation sampling" because insights "are maximised by
  exploring papers that are not characteristic of the 'average sample'"; meta-narrative uses
  "Purposive sampling of key papers" from "different research traditions"; realist synthesis
  combines comprehensive, purposive, theoretical and snowball sampling.
- **Saturation operationalised:** Downe - whether additional studies continue to reinforce the
  line of argument; O'Connell and Downe - saturation "when two articles identified late in the
  search process did not add anything new"; a "more mechanical" interpretation relates to
  "the recurrence of studies, rather than themes ... as references to the same study begin to
  reappear repeatedly."
- **Limit of saturation:** "Data or theoretical 'saturation' could have limitations in this
  context; importantly, how can a reviewer know that an additional study will not add
  important insights?"
- **Comprehensive framing:** "a comprehensive sample is problematic as it constrained by the
  number and type of resources to be searched, the diversity of materials contained within
  such sources and the time available"; Booth prefers "exhaustive" over "comprehensive".
- **When can I stop searching?:** for non-exhaustive sampling "a reviewer must develop clear
  explanations for the circumstances under which searching was terminated"; stopping rules
  proposed for methodological reviews "may apply, by extension".
- **Practice:** "81 % of published meta-ethnographies using exhaustive search strategies".
