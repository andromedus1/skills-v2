# Does the intuitive logics method - and its recent enhancements - produce "effective" scenarios? (Wright, Bradfield, Cairns, 2013)

## Source structure and scope
George Wright, Ronald Bradfield, George Cairns. Technological Forecasting and Social Change
80(4), 631-642, 2013 (Strathprints record). Engagement depth: **abstract only**; repository
states "Full text not available in this repository".

## Anchored details (abstract)
- Synthesises literature to establish common objectives of scenario interventions and
  evaluates the "well-established, but basic, 'intuitive logics' scenario development
  methodology" and its recent augmentations against them.
- Three objectives distilled: (i) "enhancing understanding: of the causal processes,
  connections and logical sequences underlying events"; (ii) "challenging conventional thinking
  in order to reframe perceptions and change the mindsets of those within organizations";
  (iii) "improving decision making: to inform strategy development".
- Finding: "a strong case for arguing that these scenario methods are designed to address two
  of the three objectives"; "other than in the augmentation in which multi-attribute value
  analysis is applied, none of the recent developments ... address directly the third
  objective".
- Conclusion: "the ubiquitous term 'scenario planning' is a misnomer".
- The abstract describes design-intent analysis ("designed to address"), not measured outcomes.
