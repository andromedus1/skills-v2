# Why we should talk about option generation in decision-making research (Kalis, Kaiser & Mojzisch 2013)

## Source structure and scope
Annemarie Kalis, Stefan Kaiser, Andreas Mojzisch. Frontiers in Psychology 4:555, published 23 August
2013, doi 10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00555. Hypothesis-and-theory article (conceptual; no new data). Engagement
depth: abstract, overview of option definitions, section on option generation and creative cognition, and
research-agenda section read.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract / Introduction:** "Most empirical studies on decision-making start from a set of given
  options for action. However, in everyday life there is usually no one asking you to choose between A,
  B, and C."
- **"Option generation: an overview":** the literature uses "option" in at least four ways differing in
  abstractness — statements relevant to goal attainment (Ward et al. 2011, police officers), "abstract
  actions" in hierarchical reinforcement learning, "steps that could be taken in solving a problem"
  (Keller & Ho 1988; Klein et al. 1995, chess moves), and fine-grained movement variants in sport (Raab
  and colleagues); "the different areas of research on option generation differ widely in focus; as said
  this makes the results of different studies on option generation hard to compare."
- **"Option generation, creative cognition, and planning":** memory retrieval "plays a substantial role"
  (citing Klein et al. 1995; Johnson & Raab 2003; Kaiser et al. 2013); in unfamiliar or complex
  situations generation is plausibly more effortful and executive, in familiar ones more automatic
  retrieval.
- **Research agenda:** Kaiser et al. (2013) reported that decisions between self-generated options "can be
  clearly distinguished on both the behavioral and the neural level from decisions between externally
  provided options"; "option generation sets the constraints for the decision-making process as a whole:
  if you do not see that a certain actual possibility is an option, you will never be able to act on this
  possibility."
