# Homogenization Effects of Large Language Models on Human Creative Ideation

## Source structure and scope
Barrett R. Anderson, Jash Hemant Shah, Max Kreminski; arXiv v2 (2024-05-10); Creativity & Cognition
2024. HTML full text read (methods, results 4.1–4.4, discussion, limitations).

## Anchored details
- **Design:** 36 participants (33 retained), within-subjects; ChatGPT (3.5) versus the Oblique Strategies
  card deck as creativity support tool; four 8-minute divergent prompts (product improvement, improbable
  consequences); told to "come up with ideas that no one else would think of"; 1,271 ideas.
- **Measures:** all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings; validated against human categorizations at 60.94%–64.63%
  agreement (GloVe 40.02%–47.25%); fluency, flexibility (181 grounded-theory categories), elaboration,
  originality (ideas produced by <5% of participants).
- **§4.1.2 Group-level homogenization:** with ChatGPT, ideas "were less divergent from the average
  embedding of all ideas generated for that task, (M = .24, SD = .07), compared to ... OS, (M = .28,
  SD = .08), t(32) = 2.154, p = 0.038, d = .47". "At the group level, ideas produced with the help of
  ChatGPT were more homogenized."
- **§4.1.3 Individual-level:** no significant difference in within-user diversity (M = .65 vs .66, p = 0.352).
- **Other facets:** ChatGPT users produced more ideas (8.39 vs 7.32, p = 0.044), covered more categories
  (8.58 vs 6.77, p = 0.001), more elaborated ideas; originality difference not significant (p = 0.108).
- **Responsibility:** participants assigned themselves less responsibility for ChatGPT-assisted ideas
  (48.17% vs 63.63%, p = 0.003).
- **Mechanism proposed:** low "inferential distance" — ChatGPT output can be adopted as a finished idea,
  whereas an Oblique Strategies card requires interpretation.
- **Limitations:** short fixed time, researcher-supplied prompts, general-purpose chatbot rather than a
  purpose-built LLM creativity tool, small sample.
