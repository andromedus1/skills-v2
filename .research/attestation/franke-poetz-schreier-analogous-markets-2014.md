# Integrating Problem Solvers from Analogous Markets in New Product Ideation (Franke, Poetz, Schreier, 2014)

## Source structure and scope
Nikolaus Franke (WU Vienna), Marion K. Poetz (Copenhagen Business School), Martin Schreier (WU
Vienna). Management Science 60(4):1063-1081, April 2014 (author-distributed copy). Read:
abstract, §1, §3 (theory), §4.1-4.2 (method, market selection), §6.1-6.2 (discussion,
limitations).

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** "most managers searching for new ideas tend to stay within their own market
  context even when they do search outside their firms' boundaries"; in "a unique symmetric
  experiment that isolates the effect of market origin ... Although solutions provided by problem
  solvers from analogous markets show lower potential for immediate use, they demonstrate
  substantially higher levels of novelty"; including analogous-market solvers "accounts for almost
  two-thirds of the well-known effect of involving lead users instead of average problem solvers";
  the effect is "further amplified when the analogous distance between the markets increases";
  it is "particularly strong in the upper tail of the novelty distribution".
- **§1 design:** 213 problem solvers from roofing, carpentry and inline skating, which share the
  analogous problem of safety-gear comfort; each participant contributed ideas to all three
  markets' problems, controlling for person-level characteristics; experts rated novelty and
  usefulness.
- **§1:** "familiarity with a problem blocks creativity and the identification of novel
  solutions".
- **§4.2 market identification:** criteria — shared analogous problem; clearly delineated
  markets; comparable importance; accessible by random sampling. Found via a pilot of secondary
  research plus 20 interviews identifying safety gear as the field, then 30 expert interviews to
  identify specific markets; final selection by fit to criteria (the markets were hand-picked).
- **§6.1 mechanisms argued:** transfer of existing solution information from the analogous market
  and lower constraints from lacking target-market knowledge.
- **§6.2 limitations:** mediating mechanism not identified; design "was not developed to measure a
  market-distance hypothesis" — distance is confounded with the younger, possibly more creative
  skater sample; one empirical context with hand-picked markets, so results "might be seen as
  somewhat idiosyncratic"; time limit may bias toward first-idea creativity; cannot say whether
  using analogous-market solvers beats prompting target-market solvers to think analogically.
