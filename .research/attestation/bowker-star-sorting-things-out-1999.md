# Sorting Things Out: Classification and Its Consequences (introduction and opening chapters, web edition)

Geoffrey C. Bowker and Susan Leigh Star. Author-hosted web edition of the introduction,
first two chapters and concluding chapters of the MIT Press book (1999, per the site's
header). Read: Introduction "To Classify is Human" (definitions section and themes) and
the passages on residual categories; later chapters only searched by keyword.

## Anchored details
- **Definition (Introduction, "Two Definitions"):** "A classification is a spatial,
  temporal or spatio-temporal segmentation of the world. A classification system is a set
  of boxes (metaphorical or literal) into which things can be put in order to then do some
  kind of work". In an "abstract, ideal sense" it has: (1) "consistent, unique
  classificatory principles in operation"; (2) "The categories are mutually exclusive";
  (3) "The system is complete … the ideal classification system provides total coverage
  of the world it describes."
- **Same section:** "No real-world working classification system that we have looked at
  meets these simple requirements and we doubt that any ever could." People "routinely mix
  together different and contradictory principles"; "mutual exclusivity may be impossible
  in practice, as when there is disagreement or ambivalence about the membership of an
  object in a category"; for completeness "there may be good reasons to ignore data that
  would make a system more comprehensive" (e.g. an anomaly too costly to introduce).
- **ICD example:** the ICD "has no single classificatory principle (it has at least four;
  which are not mutually exclusive"; it is a "statistical classification" — only
  statistically significant diseases are entered.
- **Residual categories:** the problem of "residual categories, or, the Other, or not
  elsewhere classified"; ICD rule of thumb to "distribute the residual categories. 'Not
  elsewhere classified' appears throughout the entire ICD, but nowhere as a top-level
  category" so that unavoidable uncertainty does not hit one location disproportionately.
  The "distribution of residual categories" is proposed as an analytic concept.
- **Values:** "Each standard and each category valorizes some point of view and silences
  another. This is not inherently a bad thing — indeed it is inescapable. But it is an
  ethical choice". Practical politics of classifying include "deciding what will be
  visible or invisible within the system."
