# We're Different, We're the Same: Creative Homogeneity Across LLMs

## Source structure and scope
Emily Wenger, Yoed Kenett; arXiv 2501.19361 v1 (2025-01-31), preprint. Abstract and HTML full text read
via extraction.

## Anchored details
- **Question (abstract):** whether narrowed creativity comes from using a particular LLM or from LLMs in
  general.
- **Design:** Alternate Uses Task, Forward Flow, Divergent Association Task; 22 LLMs across families (7, one
  per family, for statistics); 102 Prolific humans plus prior public datasets; all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings,
  stopwords removed.
- **Individual originality:** LLMs slightly higher than humans on AUT (0.711 vs 0.696, small effect) and DAT
  (0.801 vs 0.753); humans higher on Forward Flow (0.637 vs 0.603).
- **Population-level variability (LLM vs human):** AUT 0.459 vs 0.738 (effect 2.2); FF 0.534 vs 0.835 (2.0);
  DAT 0.665 vs 0.819 (1.4); all p < 0.001. "LLM responses are much more similar to other LLM responses than
  human responses are to each other, even after controlling for response structure".
- **System prompts:** more-creative system prompts "slightly increase individual creativity" but "response
  variability remains much lower than that of humans".
- **Limitation:** homogeneity on these tests "does not prove that LLMs in general produce homogeneous outputs".
