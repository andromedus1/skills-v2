# Function Based Design-by-Analogy: A Functional Vector Approach to Analogical Search (Murphy, Fu, Otto, Yang, Jensen & Wood)

## Source structure and scope
Jeremy Murphy, Katherine Fu, Kevin Otto, Maria Yang, Dan Jensen, Kristin Wood. Journal of Mechanical Design
(2014), DOI 10.1115/1.4028093; 16-page publisher PDF hosted by a university lab. Read: abstract, §1 background
on functional modelling, §2.3 query formulation, §3 case-study framing, §4 limitations, §5 conclusions.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** "few actual methods and tools exist to assist designers in systematically seeking and identifying
  analogies from general data sources"; the method extracts functional analogies "based on a functional basis rather
  than form or conflict descriptions", using a functional vector space model and Zipf's-law word reduction "to reduce
  ... the documents down to the applicable functionally critical terms"; two case studies "illustrate the distance range
  of analogical solutions that can be extracted. This range extends from very near-field, literal solutions to
  far-field cross-domain analogies."
- **§1:** a limitation of Otto & Wood functional modelling is that early choice of input flows yields "The single,
  domain-dependent model [that] can lead to missed opportunities for novelty and innovation"; instead "alternative
  process choices [should be] considered through higher levels of abstraction that lead to alternative functional
  models".
- **§3:** case studies — automated window-washing device and guitar pickup winder — compared "against the more
  traditional approach of simply using keyword patent searches."
- **§4:** "Significant further experimental validation is required to ensure that the methodology is indeed effective
  for multiple types of design problems"; relevancy-score weights set by a parametric process need rigorous study;
  longer patents bias toward broader function terms.
- **§5:** domain-independent search via "a complete functional vocabulary" of 1700 function terms over ≈275,000
  patents; "patents that are longer are more difficult to map analogically"; for the pickup-winder case "the search
  process extracted three of the five top analogous products".
