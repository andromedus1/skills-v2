# Comparing Analogy-Based Methods—Bio-Inspiration and Engineering-Domain Inspiration for Domain Selection and Novelty (Keshwani, Casakin and coauthors)

## Source structure and scope
Sonal Keshwani, Hernan Casakin and coauthors. Biomimetics (Basel) 2024 Jun 6; 9(6):344; PMC11201427, open
access. Read the full PMC text: abstract, §2 method, §4 results, §5 discussion, §6 conclusions.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** analogies classified by conceptual distance as "biological—(natural), cross—(distant-engineering), and
  within—(near-engineering) domain"; "twenty-six teams of novice product designers" each had one analogy from each
  domain; "biological analogies were frequently selected"; "biological-domain analogies significantly increased the
  novelty of designs compared to the within-domain ones; no significant difference was found between the biological-
  and cross-domain analogies, suggesting that middle-domain analogies can be as effective as far-domain ones."
- **§2:** 52 design master's/doctoral students with mechanical/production engineering bachelor's degrees, teams of two,
  40-minute sessions, sketches; free choice of which analogy to use for each idea.
- **§4.1:** of 136 ideas, 45.5% used biological, 29.4% cross-domain and 25% within-domain analogies.
- **§5 results list:** selection frequency for biological significantly higher than cross and within; cross vs within
  not significantly different; novelty of biological-analogy ideas significantly higher than within-domain; cross vs
  within and biological vs cross not significantly different; correlation coefficients rbio > rcross > rwithin
  numerically but not significantly different.
- **§5 interpretation:** results "contradict Ward's proposition of the path of least resistance" and differ from studies
  in which designers did not prefer far-domain analogies, possibly because here the far domain was biology (curiosity
  attributed to unfamiliarity); "the farthest-domain analogies (biological-domain) may not necessarily yield the most
  novel designs, implying that middle-domain analogies could be as effective"; engineers "showed a more significant
  ability to establish analogical correspondences with cross-domain sources than biological-domain ones" — lack of
  biology knowledge "may have affected their ability to effectively transfer".
- **§5 limitations:** short task time; novice-level experience with biological analogies; limited observations; the
  authors argue problem effects are unlikely given consistency with an earlier three-problem study.
- **§6:** recommends analogy-retrieval tools present results ordered biological, then cross-domain, then within-domain.
