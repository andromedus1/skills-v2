# Data-Driven Design-by-Analogy: State of the Art and Future Directions (Jiang, Hu, Wood & Luo)

## Source structure and scope
Shuo Jiang, Jie Hu (Shanghai Jiao Tong), Kristin L. Wood (Colorado Denver), Jianxi Luo (SUTD). arXiv 2106.01592,
dated June 1, 2021, 22 pages. Literature retrieval date stated as Feb 18, 2021; search over JMD, AI EDAM, Research
in Engineering Design, Design Science, ASME IDETC/CIE and CHI. Read: abstract, §1, §2 background (analogical
distance), §5.2–5.3 (data, applications, evaluation), future directions (benchmarks).

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** DbA "can benefit designers in mitigating design fixation and improving design ideation outcomes";
  categorises data-driven DbA studies into "analogy encoding, retrieval, mapping, and evaluation".
- **§2:** among factors studied (distance, modality, commonness, quantity, timing, similarity type), "the influence of
  the analogical distance ... has received the most attention"; summarises (as reported): Chan et al. — "far-field
  stimuli yields more novel but fewer ideas than the near-field stimuli"; Srinivasan et al. — "with the increase in the
  analogical distance, the novelty of the newly generated ideas increases, while both the quantity and quality
  decrease"; Fu et al. — "a moderate analogical distance might provide trade-offs and yield optimal design ideation
  performance." DbA efficacy "is naturally limited by the designer's prior knowledge and memory for potential retrieval
  and mapping".
- **§5.3 applications:** most tools "fall within the analogy representation and retrieval subphases"; "Although many
  AI-based methods and tools can recommend the candidate analogy list for users, few can interpret the results such as
  identifying the type of similarities"; mapping tools "have employed little machine intelligence"; "only a few
  researchers have attempted to evaluate the analogies or ideas generated by the DbA", because evaluation criteria are
  hard to set and "A traditional idea or concept in one knowledge domain might be novel in another."
- **Future directions:** "The lack of distinct ground-truth and gold-standard benchmarks in DbA-related problems makes it
  impossible to directly compare different DbA methods"; retrieval methods use different datasets and authors "often
  define their own criteria for assessing performance in the absence of ground truth". Biology-based FBS repository
  (DANE) example: a complete model "requires 40–100 hours", so the repository holds only 40 cases.
