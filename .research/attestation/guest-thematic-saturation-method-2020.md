# A simple method to assess and report thematic saturation in qualitative research

## Source structure and scope
Guest G, Namey E, Chen M. PLoS ONE 2020; 15(5): e0232076 (DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0232076,
PMC7200005). Proposes a saturation metric and validates it by bootstrapping three coded
interview datasets (40, 48, 60 interviews). Engagement depth: full text.

## Anchored details
- **Elements:** Base Size (interviews used to define the denominator of themes already found;
  tested 4, 5, 6), Run Length (consecutive interviews in which new information is counted; 2 or
  3), New Information Threshold (<=5% or 0% new themes relative to the base). Reported as e.g.
  "6+2".
- **Critique of prior metrics (Table 1 and text):** methods using the full dataset as denominator
  (Guest et al. 2006, Francis et al. 2010, Hennink et al.) are flawed because the denominator "is
  fixed, while the number of themes in the numerator gets closer to the denominator with every
  new interview considered, thus eventually reaching 100% saturation." Probability-based
  approaches (Fugard and Potts; Lowe et al.) assume a random sample; Fugard and Potts assume one
  can know in advance which themes will occur and at what rate - "not suitable for inductive
  studies". Lowe et al.: compared "four different statistically-based models, each with different
  assumptions about the degree of assumed dependency between observations".
- **Prior evidence quoted:** Guest et al. (2006 study of 60 interviews) found 70% of 114 themes in
  the first six interviews and 92% within the first 12.
- **Validation results:** at <=5% threshold saturation reached at a median of about 6 interviews
  in two datasets and 8-9 in the more heterogeneous third; "the number of themes evident across
  6–7 interviews corresponded with a median degree of saturation of 78% to 82%" of the full
  dataset's themes; "typically 6–7 interviews will capture the majority of themes in a homogenous
  sample (6 interviews to reach 80% saturation)."
- **Explicit caveat:** "As in statistical analysis—but absent the probability theory—there is no
  guarantee that saturation is in fact reached when meeting these thresholds."
- **Order effects:** interviews randomly reordered in each bootstrap resample "to offset any order
  effect"; "it is possible an important theme will emerge later", though "the most prevalent,
  high-level, themes are identified very early".
- **Scope:** "does not assume or require a random sample, nor prior knowledge of theme
  prevalence"; developed for applied inductive thematic analysis; applicability to other
  epistemological perspectives "is yet untested"; hierarchical codebooks need per-level
  calculation.
