# Investigating Effects of Stimuli on Ideation Outcomes (Srinivasan, Song, Luo, Subburaj, Elara, Blessing & Wood)

## Source structure and scope
Srinivasan Venkataraman and coauthors, Singapore University of Technology and Design. ICED17 proceedings paper,
10 pages, Design Society download. Read: abstract, background (prior near/far studies), method, results on
novelty/quality, discussion, §5.2 limitations, conclusions.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** research questions: effect of stimuli, and "effect of stimuli distance on ideation outcomes"; "105
  participants generating 226 concepts without or with patents and other resources"; findings include "(a) more
  concepts are generated with patents than without patents, (b) more concepts are generated with patents identified
  by participants on their own than using pre-chosen patents", "(d) concepts developed using both patents and other
  resources have higher novelty and quality than concepts generated without any stimuli, and (e) no significant
  correlations are observed between the proximity of stimuli to problem domains with novelty and quality of
  concepts."
- **Method:** patent technology classes placed on a knowledge-proximity network (Jaccard-based) at different distances
  from the spherical-rolling-robot domain; participants were given a week; 121 patents, one per technology class, in
  "most cited and random categories".
- **§5 discussion:** "no significant difference in average novelty between concepts generated without and with
  patents"; concepts with patents had higher average quality; concepts with any stimuli (patents, other resources,
  combination) had higher novelty and quality than with none.
- **§5.2 limitations:** patent descriptions "lengthy and described in a tedious and non-obvious manner"; browsing 121
  patents within a week "may have been cumbersome"; "the patents given to the designers are not directly related to
  the design problem, and therefore, their relevance is questionable"; undergraduate participants only.
