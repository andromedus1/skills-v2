# Taxonomy Research in Information Systems: A Systematic Assessment

Benedict Lösser, Anna Maria Oberländer, Daniel Rau. FIM discussion paper WI-930,
research paper for the 27th European Conference on Information Systems (ECIS 2019),
Stockholm–Uppsala. Full text read (16 pp.).

## Source structure and scope
Structured literature review of taxonomy/typology articles in the IS Senior Scholars'
Basket (2013–2018) plus three Decision Support Systems articles citing Nickerson et al.
(2013): 33 articles screened, 24 assessed against 19 attributes derived from and
extending Nickerson et al.'s method.

## Anchored details
- **Corpus counts (§3.1, Table 1):** taxonomy/typology articles in basket + five IS
  conferences grew from 176 (2007–2012) to 216 (2013–2018), +23%.
- **Coding reliability (§3.2):** at least two authors independently assessed each of 21
  articles; "an inter-rater agreement of 78% was obtained across all 19 final assessment
  attributes and 24 assessed articles"; "purpose beyond analysis" showed systematic
  discrepancies, resolved by redefining criteria.
- **Research product (Table 3):** MECE yes 67%, no 0%, N/A 33% (articles without
  distinct dimensions/characteristics). Dimensions: 1–4 in 50%, 5–8 in 29%, N/A 21%;
  characteristics per dimension ranged 2–34; 25% used continuous scales. Coders counted
  characteristics as collectively exhaustive when complementary (yes/no), on a continuum,
  or "entirely covering the space defined by a (explicit or implicit) meta-characteristic".
- **Process (Table 4, §4.2):** method reference: Doty & Glick 21%, Nickerson et al. 17%,
  none 58%; method clearly described 50%; iterations stated only for Nickerson-based
  articles (17%); approach deductive 63%, inductive 13%, both iteratively 25%; objects
  examined: 0 in 46%; "ending conditions were rarely specified (83%). In fact, only
  articles grounded on Nickerson et al.'s (2013) method did explicitly state and check
  ending conditions (17%)." Method clarity: half the articles did not transparently
  describe all development steps; "taxonomy development processes that explicitly followed
  Nickerson et al.'s (2013) guidance all described their research method transparently and
  comprehensibly." Example of conceptually derived taxonomy whose dimensions were
  "not explicitly justified" (Cram & Brohman 2013).
- **Evaluation (Table 5, §4.3):** 54% not evaluated; evaluation methods varied (cluster
  analysis 13%, case study 8%, others incl. robustness tests and Q-sort); "no dominating
  evaluation method"; intended users involved 13%. 75% consolidated findings into
  archetypes.
- **Discussion (§5):** "our findings on the development and evaluation of taxonomies were
  sobering"; root causes proposed: method design possibly "too detailed and complex",
  lacking actionable practices "for example with regards to the examination of objects";
  "the IS body of knowledge lacks any methodological guidance on taxonomy evaluation".
- **Limitations (§6):** journals only (no conference papers); excluded "framework" and
  "classification" synonyms; assessment relied on reported information only — "We could
  not assess what was actually done by the researchers."
