# Checking reference lists to find additional studies for systematic reviews

## Source structure and scope
Horsley T, Dingwall O, Sampson M. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews 2011, MR000026 (DOI
10.1002/14651858.mr000026.pub2). Cochrane methodology review. Engagement depth: full structured
abstract.

## Anchored details (abstract)
- "To date, no systematic review has explicitly examined the effectiveness of checking reference
  lists as a method to supplement electronic searching."
- Effectiveness defined as "the proportion of relevant studies identified by review authors
  solely by checking reference lists".
- 12 studies (13 publications) included; "interpretability and generalizability of these
  studies is difficult and the study designs used were at high risk of bias."
- "The additional yield ... of relevant studies identified through checking reference lists
  ranged from 2.5% to 42.7%." No cost data reported.
- Conclusions: "some evidence to support the use of checking reference lists ... However, this
  evidence is derived from weak study designs." Where identification through handsearching and
  database searching "is difficult, it would seem prudent" to check reference lists; "The
  challenge, therefore, is for review authors to recognize those situations."
