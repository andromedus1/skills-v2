# Card Sorting: How Many Users to Test

Jakob Nielsen, Nielsen Norman Group, dated July 18, 2004 (as shown on the page).
Engagement depth: page fetched through a model-mediated extractor; figures and quoted
phrases below are as returned from the page, not read from the original Tullis & Wood
conference paper (which this page reports on).

## Anchored details
- **Study reported (cite-through):** Nielsen reports that Tullis and Wood tested 168 users
  in a card sort and analysed random subsets to simulate smaller studies, comparing
  similarity scores (how consistently users group item pairs) with the full-sample result.
- **Correlations with full sample (as reported):** 5 users 0.75; 15 users 0.90; 20 users
  about 0.93; 30 users 0.95; 60 users 0.98.
- **Recommendation:** "Test 15 users" for most projects; 30 for high-stakes projects;
  0.90 "is a more comfortable place to stop"; 30 users giving 0.95 is "usually not worth
  twice the money".
- **Rationale:** card sorting is generative; "there is great variability in different
  people's mental models", so more users are needed for "a stable picture".
