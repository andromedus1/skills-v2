# Logical division

2016-09-06, last edited 2024-07-10; also published as Knowledge Organization 43(7):
539–549 (per the entry's colophon). Full entry read.

## Source structure and scope
§1 kinds of division; §2 basics; §3 history; §4 formalization; §5 the rules; §6 status of
the rules; §7 the process of logical division; §8 conclusion.

## Anchored details
- **§5 Toohey's (1918) four rules:** RULE I dividing members "mutually exclusive as regards
  the basis employed" (exclusivity, "pairwise disjoint"); RULE II dividing members
  "together be co-extensive with the class or genus which is divided" (exhaustivity,
  "coverage"); RULE III "Each process of division must have only one basis" — mixing bases
  (nationality and vocation; London Education Classification's teenager/adult/older
  person/parent/housewife) produces classes that happen to be exclusive only as "a somewhat
  fragile arrangement" that a new case can break; RULE IV divide into proximate species
  ("division should not make a leap"). Too many exclusive children yield always-empty
  classes; too few leave members unclassified.
- **§6:** Rules I and II are the "JEPD" (Jointly Exclusive Pairwise Distinct) condition.
  Aristotelian division "failed" for biological species because "members of a biological
  species often possessed some, but not all, of a manifold of characteristics — there was
  no single property possessed by all members" (motivating cladistics). Natural vs
  artificial kinds: division works best with artificial kinds devised for a purpose.
- **§7:** repeated bifurcation (B / not-B) guarantees exclusivity and exhaustivity but
  yields "a tall narrow tree" that distorts genus/species ("bifurcation is of limited
  use"). "In areas like logic or mathematics, it often can be known that conditions are
  exclusive and/or exhaustive … However, in science or in the observable empirical world,
  it is hard to have such certainty". Catch-all "everything else" class secures
  exhaustivity; Mills (2004) quoted: "The obvious difficulty encountered here is that of
  our imperfect knowledge", remedied technically by dichotomy or a residual "Others"
  class. Many library classifications (e.g. DDC 820 for Keats's works) do not satisfy JEPD.
- **§8 Conclusion:** division "can work well in the realm of logic, mathematics, and other
  non-empirical and a priori areas. But the realm of the empirical is more challenging";
  best potential with "completely artificial classifications" devised for a purpose. Facets
  as multiple division trees on the same class used simultaneously (jeweller example: price
  tree and gemstone tree). The single-shared-property basis has been criticised by Rosch's
  prototype theory and Wittgenstein's family resemblances; others defend it.
