# Improving the Generation of Decision Objectives (Bond, Carlson & Keeney 2010)

## Source structure and scope
Samuel D. Bond, Kurt A. Carlson, Ralph L. Keeney. Decision Analysis 7(3), September 2010, pp. 238–255,
doi 10.1287/deca.1100.0172. A self-report pretest, a reanalysis of Bond et al. (2008) data, and three
experiments testing interventions on objective generation. Engagement depth: full text read through
§7.2 (prescriptive implications); conclusion skimmed.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** two impediments — "not thinking broadly enough about the range of relevant objectives,
  and not thinking deeply enough to articulate every objective within the range that is considered."
  Interventions: sample objectives, organization by category, direct challenges with or without a
  warning that important objectives are missing. "The use of category names and direct challenges with a
  warning both led to improvements in the quantity of objectives generated without impacting their
  quality; other interventions yielded less improvement."
- **§3.1 (pretest, N=94 poster decision, 36-item list):** of 78 usable explanations, ~36% "too shallow",
  ~49% "too narrow"; 92% acknowledged their generated list was incomplete.
- **§3.2 (reanalysis):** master-list objectives split into four mutually exclusive, collectively
  exhaustive conceptual categories; self-generation concentrated in each participant's top category
  (e.g. Study 1: 2.60 in top category vs 1.84 under a random-generation model, t(61)=9.69; 0.58 in lowest
  vs 1.36 predicted, t(61)=-8.56) — "breadth of thinking was insufficient."
- **§4.1 (Study 1, dissertation topic, n=20 doctoral students):** unaided list 7.25; a second,
  category-structured opportunity (five categories) added 4.5; master list of 34 items; checked 21.9;
  self-generated 9.6 vs recognized 12.3. Objectives added at the category stage that mapped uniquely:
  2.9, increasing self-generated set by over 30%. Added objectives' importance 7.3 vs initial 7.7 (ns).
  Authors note it is unclear how much of the benefit came from categories vs the "second chance."
- **§4.2 (Study 2, MBA program, n=158 usable of 194, three-way between-subjects):** listed 9.2 standard,
  12.7 categories, 12.2 examples (10.45 adjusted for four provided examples); categories > standard
  (F(1,155)=16.00) and > examples. Recognized (missed) objectives: 7.9 standard, 10.8 examples, 7.9
  categories — examples condition missed reliably more (F(1,155)=8.26). Interpretation: consistent with
  part-list cueing, sample objectives "may also hinder generation by anchoring respondents on the
  specific examples provided"; upfront categories led to "more specific (but not more complete) sets."
- **§5 (Study 3, internship, n=295; 154 undecided, 141 decided; six randomized second-pass
  interventions):** initial list 6.39. Mean objectives added (Table 1): simple review 0.55 (20% added any);
  deeper review [warning that people typically list <50% and missed objectives are as important] 2.77
  (87%); add three 3.08; add six 3.90; add nine 4.87; categories (+warning) 3.56. Target size regression
  beta=0.30, F(1,145)=9.27, p<0.01. Categories not reliably better than deeper review (p=0.134): "for
  individuals already motivated to think more deeply, the benefit of categories was minimal."
- **§5.2.3 (quality):** importance of added vs initial objectives did not differ (7.23 vs 7.28,
  t(465)=0.32, p=0.76); "the interventions did not succeed by stimulating trivial objectives."
- **§6 summary:** a template of approximately 30 possible objectives, "even when viewed after successful
  interventions, allowed individuals to identify many additional relevant objectives."
- **§7.1:** individuals "typically generated 30% to 50% of their relevant objectives on their first
  attempt"; a reasonable challenge target is to "approximately double" the initial number. Templates, if
  they exist, should be consulted only after independent deliberation.
- **§7.2 (groups):** each individual generates independently (and tries to double) before group
  discussion, because "communication may redirect individuals' from their own ideas to those that they
  hear expressed"; final set is the union of individual lists, de-duplicated.
