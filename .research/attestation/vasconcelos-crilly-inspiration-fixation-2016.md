# Inspiration and fixation: Questions, methods, findings, and challenges (Vasconcelos & Crilly)

## Source structure and scope
Luis A. Vasconcelos, Nathan Crilly (Cambridge Engineering Design Centre). Design Studies 42 (2016) 1–32, open
access. Methods-oriented review of 25 closely examined experimental studies. Read: abstract, §1 (fixation
definitions, common procedure), §2.1 stimulus variables (number, proximity, timing), §2.2 process variables
(problem abstraction, instructions), §4 discussion and recommendations. Note: this review is a cite-through for
the primary studies it summarises; those studies are reported here as attributed by these authors.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** 25 studies reveal "14 manipulated variables"; "when scrutinised and compared, they show great
  variety in the methods used and the results obtained"; offers a methodological critique.
- **§1 (p.3):** Jansson & Smith (1991) showed an example solution "reduced the designers' propensity to move
  effectively between the conceptual space (of abstract ideas) and the configuration space"; fixation was
  identified when example features — sometimes "contradicting the brief" — were incorporated. Interpretations of
  fixation have been narrowed and broadened; "fixation is framed as an unfavourable phenomenon" in most studies.
- **§1 (p.4) common procedure:** participants "(often students)" work individually; "limited time ... (often an
  hour or less)"; simple problems; metrics either relatively objective (number of concepts, number of different
  types, repetition of example features) or subjective (novelty, feasibility).
- **§2.1.3 number of stimuli (pp.7–8):** Perttula & Liikkanen — participants shown four designs on different
  principles "seldom included solutions from the categories represented", but no significant differences vs
  control; Perttula & Sipilä — number of examples had no influence; Dahl & Moreau — more examples "did not
  increase the number of seemingly distant analogies"; Sio et al. suggest several examples "can actually further
  inhibit creativity"; "it is not clear how many stimuli to present".
- **§2.1.4 proximity (p.8):** no clear boundaries for near/far, but "good support for the idea that stimuli that are
  neither too near nor too far from the problem domain are more likely to produce creative insights" (citing Chan
  et al. 2011, Dahl & Moreau 2002, Fu et al. 2013 and others); "when there are no directive hints, analogous
  thought is not likely to be enhanced (... Gick & Holyoak, 1980, 1983), indicating that spontaneous analogical
  transfer is not simple"; "research has only offered general suggestions for what this distance is."
- **§2.1.7 timing (p.9):** Tseng et al. (2008) — "distantly related stimuli impact more positively the design process
  when it has already started while near ones have more effect before any design activity"; Perttula & Liikkanen —
  more categories explored when stimuli are presented mid-process.
- **§2.2.3 problem abstraction (p.13):** "problem abstraction is required to draw analogies between dissimilar
  domains"; Zahner et al. (2010) — abstract problems promote divergence, concrete ones convergence; "if a problem is
  framed in terms that are too abstract, then alternatives are less likely to fit the problem requirements, and if
  it is too concrete the solutions become similar and converge within a domain"; absent context can trigger "a
  contextual defaulting strategy, therefore leading to self-similar solutions"; "the most appropriate balance ...
  is not clear."
- **§2.2.4 instructions (pp.13–14):** Chrysikou & Weisberg — warning about negative features was not enough;
  explicit avoid-instructions eliminated fixation but increased task difficulty; Jansson & Smith and Perttula &
  Sipilä found fixation despite instructions; Smith et al. (1993) — telling participants to diverge "did not
  decrease the participants' conformity"; "research has been equivocal on the benefits of constraining
  instructions".
- **§4 (pp.26–27):** recommends standardised reporting and non-experimental methods; questions "whether the
  constrained behaviour measured in the experiments is really negative"; Sio et al. suggest fixation "may restrict
  the variety of solutions" yet may enhance quality and novelty; Viswanathan et al. propose some fixation could be
  beneficial in later design stages.
