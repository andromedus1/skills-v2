# Experimental Assessment of TRIZ Effectiveness in Idea Generation (Vargas Hernandez, Schmidt, Okudan Kremer 2012)

## Source structure and scope
Noe Vargas Hernandez (UTEP), Linda C. Schmidt (Maryland), Gul E. Okudan Kremer (Penn State); ASEE
Annual Conference 2012, paper AC 2012-4512; 13 pages read in full. Sections: Abstract; 1 Introduction;
2 Background (2.1 Analogies, 2.2 Creative ideation using TRIZ); 3 Experimental methodology; 4 Setup;
5 Assessment (genealogy tree, quantity, novelty, variety); 6 Results; 7 Conclusions. (A journal version,
"Systematic Ideation Effectiveness Study of TRIZ", J. Mechanical Design 2013, was not fetched.)

## Anchored details
- **§2 (TRIZ as described):** Altshuller "identified patterns of innovation in devices, articulated
  heuristic approaches ... proposed an overall algorithm (called 'ARIZ')"; "The portion of the TRIZ
  methodology used most frequently by instructors is the contradiction matrix": "39 engineering characteristics" to define problems, "40 inventive principles"
  generalized from patents; the matrix "directs a designer to apply up to four different inventive
  principles ... to overcome any identified contradiction". Authors attribute TRIZ's market to being
  "based on patents (implying successful novelty)" and giving "clear direction".
- **§2.1 (as reported):** Chan et al. (153 engineering seniors) found analogies from more distant domains
  "more likely to inspire novelty ... but would reduce the number of ideas generated".
- **§2.2 (as reported):** Okudan et al. found improved novelty, variety and quality with TRIZ plus
  brainstorming; Howard et al. found TRIZ principles "encouraged the designers to find less obvious
  ideas".
- **§3 design:** one factor (TRIZ present/absent); treatment = a PowerPoint lecture plus handouts of
  principles and contradiction matrix; control = no formal method. Responses: Quantity, Novelty ("How
  unusual or unexpected an idea is as compared to other ideas"), Variety ("How different concepts are
  from each other"), per Shah et al. 2003. Subjects: UTEP 21 control / 9 TRIZ (seniors, individual); UMD
  20 control / 21 TRIZ (graduate, individual); Penn State teams (graduate). Task: traffic-light redesign.
- **§5.1 genealogy tree:** ideas categorized from a common function through Physical Principles (PP) and
  Working Principles (WP); trees from groups are merged so branch names align. **§5.4 Variety** weights
  PP branches 9 and WP branches 3 — "branches at higher levels have more weight". Novelty for a WP branch
  is based on the share of all ideas falling in it (a rarity measure; the printed formula carries an
  editor comment "Numerator is inverted").
- **§5 note:** text says "only those ideas from ideation session 1 (after the intervention) were
  assessed" although §4 lists Session 1 as before the intervention — an internal inconsistency in the
  paper.
- **§6 results:** merged genealogy trees "shared most of the terms on branch even before merging".
  Quantity — UTEP control 2.05 vs TRIZ 1.667 (p=0.2879); UMD 4.10 vs 3.80 (p=0.5409); Penn State 15.0 vs
  16.286 (p=0.6291). Novelty — UTEP 0.241 vs 0.560 (p=0.0004); UMD 0.095 vs 0.186 (p=0.0145); Penn State
  0.683 vs 0.651 (p=0.5786). Variety (no significance test reported) — UTEP 1.610 vs 4.200; UMD 0.805 vs
  1.111; Penn State 0.912 vs 0.920.
- **§7 conclusions:** "TRIZ improves some of the metrics while worsening others"; "some of the results
  are not statistically significant at the standard p=0.05 level, they indicate a trend"; outcome "is
  dependent on the involved variables: subject, design task, design method, and other environmental
  variables".
