# What rationales drive architectural decisions? An empirical inquiry (Borowa et al. 2023)

## Source structure and scope
Klara Borowa, Rafał Lewańczyk, Klaudia Stpiczyńska, Patryk Stradomski, Andrzej Zalewski (Warsaw
University of Technology); arXiv 2309.14164v1, 25 Sep 2023; 16 pages read via text extraction
(abstract, §1–2, results on rationale origins, §6 threats to validity). Mixed-method: questionnaire (63
participants) and 13 interviews; self-reported rationales.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** "architects prefer to choose solutions which are familiar to them or that guarantee fast
  software implementation. Mid-career architects (5 to 15 years of experience) are more open to new
  solutions than senior and junior practitioners."
- **§2 background:** "The traditional decision-making process, which includes listing all possible
  alternatives and their attributes, is impractical for software design decisions ... because of the
  number of possible architectural solutions"; "an entirely rational design-making process is impossible
  as long as it depends on human beings".
- **Rationale origins (interview coding, occurrences/interviews):** "Practitioner's experience (58/13)" —
  beginners "preferred the solutions which they had used previously"; experts "did not feel the need to
  try new solutions and leave their comfort zone"; mid-career practitioners "most likely to possess the
  knowledge and willingness to discover new solutions". Using a "decision-making methodology" as rationale
  was reported only by experienced practitioners.
- **"Familiarity with a particular solution (25/9)":** "Since it is rarely possible to explore all the
  possible alternatives, prior experiences are the primary source of information".
- **§6 threats:** self-report methods; "participants' actual rationale may differ from those that they
  reported ... they may be impacted by cognitive biases ... that they are not aware of."
