# Don't overdo that MECE thing – make your ideas independent and collectively exhaustive

Arnaud Chevallier (byline "Professor @ IMD"), Powerful Problem Solving blog, dated
Jun 14, 2012 (part 3 of a 4-part series on MECE thinking; references Chevallier 2016,
Strategic Thinking in Complex Problem Solving, OUP, pp. 58–67). Full post read
(two figures missing from the fetched page). Practitioner/advocacy argument; no data.

## Anchored details
- "ME stands for 'mutually exclusive,' or 'no overlaps'; CE stands for 'collectively
  exhaustive,' or 'no gaps.' The spirit of the guideline 'think MECE' is consider all
  possible answers exactly once"; "no overlaps" is "primarily a measure of efficiency".
- The textbook consulting breakdown "increase revenues / decrease costs" is CE but "isn't
  ME, because you can attempt to increase revenues and also attempting to decrease costs";
  the options are "independent" (defined as "distinct": doing one node does not require
  doing another), not mutually exclusive.
- Forcing true ME ("only revenues" / "only costs") loses CE unless a hybrid branch is
  added, and the hybrid "re-tangled together the two elements … So, I'd contend that that
  new structure is a lot less insightful". Proposal: ICE — "independent and collectively
  exhaustive".
- "Sometimes you don't want to be ME": redundancies "can be desirable, or, indeed,
  necessary" (Landau 1969 on redundancy; single-sensor 737 MAX MCAS example); "We like
  redundancies, as long as they're intentional" (attributed to Paula Sanders).
