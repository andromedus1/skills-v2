# MECE principle (Wikipedia)

## Anchored details
- **Lead:** MECE "is a grouping principle for separating a set of items into subsets that
  are mutually exclusive (ME) and collectively exhaustive (CE)"; developed in the late
  1960s by Barbara Minto at McKinsey & Company, underlying the Minto Pyramid Principle;
  Minto says the idea goes back to Aristotle. Non-MECE example: nationality (dual
  nationality breaks ME; stateless people break CE).
- **Common uses:** consultants break client problems into "logical, clean buckets of
  analysis" handed out as work streams.
- **Criticisms:** "criticized for not being exhaustive, as it does not exclude
  superfluous/extraneous items"; "can be too limiting as mutual exclusiveness is not
  necessarily desirable … forcing the answers themselves to be MECE can be unnecessarily
  limiting"; "by definition, it precludes redundancies. However, there are cases where
  redundancies are desirable or even necessary."
- **Historical antecedents:** Ranganathan (1937, Prolegomena to Library Classification):
  Canon of Exhaustiveness and Canon of Exclusiveness.
