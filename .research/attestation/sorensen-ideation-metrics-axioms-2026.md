# Axiom-based aggregation functions for calculating variety, novelty, quality and quantity of ideation results (Sorensen et al. 2026)

## Source structure and scope
Carl D. Sorensen, Thomas J. Ashworth, Tyler Stapleton, Christopher A. Mattson (BYU), Michael L. Anderson
(USAF Academy). Design Science, published online 23 February 2026, doi 10.1017/dsj.2026.10051. Full HTML
text read (abstract, literature review, axioms §5, design trees §6, discussion). Methodological paper
proposing metric axioms; it also restates the definitions of Shah, Smith & Vargas-Hernandez (2003)
("SVS").

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** SVS criteria "can help design teams understand the thoroughness of their ideation work
  and can help design researchers compare the performance of different ideation methods. However,
  existing methods for aggregating these metrics to obtain total set scores ... are problematic."
- **§3 Literature review — SVS definitions as quoted:** "Novelty is a measure of how unusual or
  unexpected an idea is as compared to other ideas." "Variety is a measure of the explored solution space
  during the idea generation process." "Quantity is the total number of ideas generated." "Quality ... is
  the measure of the feasibility of an idea and how close it comes to meet[ing] the design
  specifications."
- **§3 — SVS limitations listed:** "The lack of a method to calculate a novelty score for a set"; "The
  lack of a method for determining when two ideas are insufficiently different to be counted
  separately"; "The inability to determine how much an individual idea adds to the variety of the set";
  "The calculated variety is divided by the number of ideas in the set, so in some sense it is the
  'average' variety"; novelty "requires a consistent functional decomposition for all the ideas".
- **§5.5.1 — SVS variety worked example:** genealogy-tree levels weighted 10/6/3/1 (physical principle,
  working principle, embodiment, detail); 11-idea set scores 72/11 = 6.54; removing one detail-level idea
  gives 71/10 = 7.1 — "The SVS variety score has increased as a result of removing an idea, which violates
  Axiom V-1." Average novelty (used in later work) also fails their novelty axioms.
- **§5.1 axioms of variety:** "Axiom V-1: Adding a unique entry at any level of abstraction should
  increase the total variety ... removing a unique entry ... should reduce the total variety". "Axiom V-2:
  Adding a unique entry at a higher level of abstraction should increase the total variety more than
  adding a unique entry at a lower level." "Axiom V-3: Adding a unique entry ... that is similar to many
  other ideas should increase the total variety less than adding a unique entry at the same abstraction
  level that is different from other ideas." Quantity axiom U-1: duplicates "should not be counted again".
- **§6 OPED tree levels:** Objective (what, not how; common to all ideas) → Principle ("A top-level
  statement of how the objective is to be achieved ... general enough that there can be multiple ways of
  embodying the principle"; fill-in: "An idea could achieve the objective by ____") → Embodiment
  (tangible, sketchable) → Detail ("adds to, but does not fundamentally change, the embodiment"). Example:
  objective "Obtain an ice-free windshield"; principles "Remove ice", "Melt ice", "Prevent ice formation",
  "Prevent ice adhesion"; embodiments "Ice scraper", "Windshield cover". "The decision between embodiment
  and detail is partly subjective." "if the team has not explicitly placed the ideas into a tree structure,
  it is unlikely that the idea elements will properly complete the design tree."
- **§6.2:** "variety is a measure of how well the ideas explore the design space and novelty is a measure
  of how well the ideas expand the design space." "We recognize that the OPED tree cannot describe the
  total design space. It can only describe the explored space ... the OPED trees can only produce relative
  measures". "Closely related ideas will overlap one another in the design space, so the amount of space
  explored per idea is less than for distantly related ideas."
- **Discussion:** "there is no absolute scale for determining the variety and novelty of an idea set ...
  the metrics cannot yet be used to say whether a set has 'enough' novelty or variety"; this "could
  potentially help ... decide whether it is time to move on from the ideation stage" (future work). "Both
  the variety and novelty scores are correlated with the quantity of ideas." Evaluated sets of over 2000
  ideas from more than 15 teams; "A set of 2000 ideas can be evaluated in less than 100 hours". "There is
  no inherent 'perfect' tree structure, so different evaluators will have different trees. The effects of
  such differences on the idea set scores have not yet been evaluated". Increased variation with multiple
  raters observed anecdotally, not statistically analysed.
