# Saturation in qualitative research: exploring its conceptualization and operationalization

## Source structure and scope
Saunders B, Sim J, Kingstone T, Baker S, Waterfield J, Bartlam B, Burroughs H, Jinks C. Quality &
Quantity 2018 (DOI 10.1007/s11135-017-0574-8; PMC5993836). Conceptual/critical paper drawing on published examples. Engagement depth: full
text read.

## Anchored details
- **Definition in use:** saturation "is commonly taken to indicate that, on the basis of the data
  that have been collected or analysed hitherto, further data collection and/or analysis are
  unnecessary."
- **Four models (Table 1):** theoretical saturation (development of theoretical categories;
  focus: sampling); inductive thematic saturation (emergence of new codes or themes; focus:
  analysis); a priori thematic saturation (degree to which predetermined codes are exemplified;
  focus: sampling); data saturation (degree to which new data repeat earlier data; focus: data
  collection).
- **Glaser and Strauss (quoted):** the researcher "goes out of his way to look for groups that
  stretch diversity of data as far as possible, just to make certain that saturation is based on
  the widest possible range of data on the category."
- **Code vs meaning (citing Hennink et al. 2017):** saturation of codes was achieved earlier than
  saturation of "dimensions, nuances, or insights"; an approach relying only on number of codes
  "misses the point of saturation".
- **Event vs degree:** Strauss and Corbin treat saturation as "a matter of degree" with always
  "the potential for 'the "new" to emerge'"; the authors argue it "can only be a matter of the
  analyst's decision—saturation is an ongoing, cumulative judgment that one makes, and perhaps
  never completes".
- **Equivocation in practice:** studies claim saturation then collect more interviews "to
  'confirm'" it; studies combine saturation with a priori sample sizes; Mason's observation that
  interview sample sizes are commonly multiples of ten.
- **Uncertain logic:** saturation is "essentially a statement about the unobserved ... based on
  the observed"; for negative case analysis "the logic becomes more tenuous—a statement about the
  unobserved based on the unobserved."
- **Circularity:** claiming saturation demonstrates trustworthiness of coding is questioned
  because "saturation will be a direct and automatic consequence of one's coding decisions".
- **Conclusion:** saturation should be operationalised consistently with the research question
  and analytic approach, considered "as a matter of degree", with "some limit to its scope".
