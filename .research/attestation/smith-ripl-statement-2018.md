# Reporting Items for Patent Landscapes (RIPL): The RIPL Statement (Smith, Arshad, Trippe, Collins, Brindley, Carr)

## Source structure and scope
James A. Smith, Zeeshaan Arshad, Anthony Trippe (Patinformatics), Gary S. Collins, David A.
Brindley, Andrew J. Carr (Oxford and other institutions). Author manuscript, 13 pages, read in
full: abstract; introduction with Box 1 (evidence of poor reporting); checklist development
(modified Delphi); definition; Table 1 checklist; discussion; scope; limitations.

## Anchored details
- **Box 1 (summarising the authors' prior systematic review):** 81 full-text articles with patent
  landscapes assessed against a 20-item checklist; "No articles complied with all criteria and
  mean compliance to the checklist was 64%. Just eight articles (9.9%) reported all
  methodological items that would enable replication and validation of the study." Reporting
  quality did not correlate with SCImago journal ranking.
- **Checklist development:** modified Delphi (late 2016-early 2017), 70 experts invited, 20
  agreed (29%), 18 completed round two; 57 candidate items -> 48 consensus items -> consolidated
  21-item checklist.
- **Consensus definition:** "A landscape is an analysis of the relationships between multiple
  sets of indicators or of those indicators measured against temporal, technical or spatial
  dimensions. At least one of the indicators, in the case of a patent landscape, are patent
  publications or some aspect thereof. A landscape seeks to encompass an entire population of
  relevant data, rather than a random sample drawn from that population".
- **Table 1 items relevant to scope and coverage:** item 5 Search — databases and offices
  searched, dates, components of patents searched, all search terms; item 6 selection criteria;
  item 7 how identified patents were sorted for relevance; item 11 numbers assessed, included and
  reasons for exclusion at each stage (flow diagram); item 15 list of patent numbers; item 17
  limitations "in the context of the reliability of the conclusions", including "how sources of
  error were reduced".
- **Discussion:** "the process of conducting a systematic review is analogous to that of
  conducting a patent landscape", but landscape articles are often published as other article
  types and "may avoid the close methodological scrutiny" of a systematic review.
- **Scope:** "this guidance does not extend to recommendations regarding methodologies; rather,
  it focusses specifically on reporting"; refers to the WIPO "Guidelines for Preparing Patent
  Landscape Reports" for methodology.
- **Limitations:** no face-to-face meeting; expert recruitment partly by author recommendation
  "could introduce bias"; outcome depends on participants' expertise. The evidence base for need
  was life-sciences landscapes.
- RIPL contains no item requiring a recall/coverage estimate (inferred from reading Table 1:
  items cover search reporting and limitations, not a measured completeness figure).
