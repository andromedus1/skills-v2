# Cognitive benefits of scenario planning: Its impact on biases and decision quality (Meissner and Wulf, 2013)

## Source structure and scope
Philip Meissner and Torsten Wulf. Technological Forecasting and Social Change, vol. 80, issue
4, pp. 801-814, 2013 (bibliographic record on RePEc/IDEAS). Engagement depth: **abstract
only** via the RePEc record; experimental design, task, measures and effect sizes not read.

## Anchored details (abstract)
- Experimental study with 252 graduate management students.
- Reported finding: "scenario planning reduces the framing bias and that it has a more positive
  effect on decision quality than tools traditionally used in strategic planning."
- Framed as empirical support for claimed cognitive advantages of scenario planning.
- Participants were students, not practising strategists (population as stated in the
  abstract); transfer to expert practice is not addressed in the abstract.
