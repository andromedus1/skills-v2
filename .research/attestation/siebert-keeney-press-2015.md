# Entscheiden ohne Tunnelblick – Wie sich die kreative Suche nach Alternativen lohnt (Universität Bayreuth press release 168/2015)

## Source structure and scope
University of Bayreuth press release no. 168 (2015), German, 6 pages, read in full. Reports the
Siebert & Keeney Operations Research (2015) studies (doi 10.1287/opre.2015.1411). Secondary institutional
report of the paper's results; numbers below are as reported by the press office, not read off the paper.
Translations below are paraphrases from German.

## Anchored details
- **p.2:** In a study with about 200 bachelor/master business students deciding on an internship, they
  identified on their own only "37 Prozent" of the alternatives they later rated relevant when shown a
  comprehensive "Master-Liste" of options. Before seeing the master list, only "44 Prozent" had considered
  the option they subsequently rated best, and only "10 Prozent" had identified their top three in
  advance.
- **p.2 example:** for a student intern wanting a full-time job, most respondents named options such as
  working longer or taking on responsibility, while the most promising option — asking the boss what to
  do in the second half of the internship — did not occur to them.
- **p.3:** in further tests with about 400 students, those who first articulated their objectives found
  "doppelt so viele" (twice as many) alternatives as those who had not; in "58 Prozent" of cases the set
  identified with objectives in mind was rated higher afterwards.
- **p.4:** a comprehensive master list of objectives combined with instructions to use them for search
  increased the number of alternatives considered by "53 Prozent". Giving more time was reported as not a
  substitute for systematically articulating objectives ("Mehr Zeit ist kein Ersatz").
- **p.4:** recommendation that multiple people (decision makers, analysts, other stakeholders)
  participate in identifying objectives and alternatives.
