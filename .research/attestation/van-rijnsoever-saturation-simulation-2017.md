# (I Can't Get No) Saturation: A simulation and guidelines for sample sizes in qualitative research

## Source structure and scope
Frank J. van Rijnsoever (Utrecht University; INGENIO). *PLoS ONE* 12(7): e0181689, published July 26, 2017,
doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0181689 (as printed). Simulation study of the number of information sources needed
until every code in a population has been observed at least once, under three sampling scenarios, across 1,100
hypothetical populations (11–101 codes; mean code-observation probability 0.09–0.91). Engagement depth: full
text read except S1 Appendix.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** "Theoretical saturation is reached after all the codes in the population have been observed once in
  the sample." Scenarios: "random chance" (probability sampling), "minimal information" (at least one new code per
  sampling step), "maximum information" (largest number of new codes per step). "theoretical saturation is more
  dependent on the mean probability of observing codes than on the number of codes in a population"; purposive
  scenarios "are significantly more efficient than random chance, but yield fewer repetitions per code to validate the
  findings."
- **Model assumptions:** "each code represents only one piece of information and that two different codes are not allowed
  to represent the same information ... synonyms are removed"; the number of codes in the population "is unknown" at the
  start.
- **What observation probability represents:** depends on "the likelihood of an information source actually containing the
  code, the willingness and ability of the source ... to let the code be uncovered, and the ability of the researcher to
  observe the code"; less researcher experience lowers it; "Having multiple independent coders, on the other hand, can
  increase the probability of observing a code."
- **Repetitive codes:** codes observed more than once "can help guard against misinformation"; a code still observed only
  once after many steps warrants "critical examination", though "A frequency of one does not mean that the code is wrong by
  definition".
- **Results (Fig. 2):** with 101 codes and mean observation probability below 0.1, random chance "generally requires more than
  1000 sampling steps to reach theoretical saturation in 95% of the cases", versus about 46 sources (minimal information) and
  about 20 (maximal information); at higher probabilities random and minimal-information scenarios converge.
- **Results (Fig. 3):** maximal-information sampling "only yields between 1 and 3 observations per code. This low number of
  codes makes the use of repetitive codes for maximal information very limited"; "a clear trade-off between the efficiency of
  the scenario and the number of repetitive codes". Requiring each code twice adds about 10% more steps under random chance.
- **Limitations:** scenarios are idealised; one population simulated per parameter combination; population sizes not varied.
- **Guidelines:** "not intended as formal mechanistic rules"; guideline 1: identify the population of information sources and
  its sub-populations and argue the basis for distinguishing them, updating the delineation as sampling proceeds.
