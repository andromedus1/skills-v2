# The process for individuating TRIZ Inventive Principles: deterministic, stochastic or domain-oriented? (Borgianni et al. 2021)

## Source structure and scope
Yuri Borgianni (Bozen-Bolzano), Lorenzo Fiorineschi, Francesco Saverio Frillici, Federico Rotini
(Florence). Design Science vol. 7, 2021, e12, doi 10.1017/dsj.2021.12, open access (CC BY-NC-SA). Full
HTML text read: Abstract, §1 Introduction, §2 Related work, §3 Materials and methods, §4 Results, §5
Discussion, §6 Conclusions. Authors are TRIZ researchers (each "more than 10 years of experience").

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** "practitioners diffusedly experience difficulties in the selection of the most suitable
  tool"; Inventive Principles (IPs) with the Contradiction Matrix are "the most popular TRIZ technique";
  "the reliability of the Contradiction Matrix is often questioned and an agreement on a solid and
  reliable procedure for the selection of IPs is far from being reached." Finding: IPs used are
  "considerably related with the technical-scientific domain and the nature of the problem".
- **§1:** cites Vargas-Hernandez, Schmidt & Okudan 2013 as experimental support for TRIZ effectiveness;
  cites Ilevbare, Probert & Phaal (2013) as highlighting "the lack of rules and procedures to select
  proper tools" (as attributed). "This indeterminateness ... might be caused by the fact that TRIZ has not
  been developed following the standard procedures of scientific validation" (citing Tomiyama et al. 2009;
  Chechurin & Borgianni 2016); "the actual usefulness of such a large set of tools is argued in the
  scholarly debate."
- **§2:** TRIZ originates from analysing inventive solutions and regularities; scholars attribute
  "supposed existence of logic and repeatable processes in the problem-solution path in the Contradiction
  Matrix". IPs' abstraction gives general applicability but "causes usability challenges". "IPs'
  usefulness for beginners and practitioners is overwhelmingly more addressed in the literature than their
  limitations are."
- **§3 method:** sample of convenience of 161 TRIZ-solved problems from 53 TRIZ Future Conference papers
  (2015–2019), excluding a-posteriori justification examples; classification by author consensus. Using
  the 39 engineering parameters would give n(n−1)/2 = 741 contradiction combinations, "plainly
  incompatible with the quantity of available case studies". χ² tests of IP distribution.
- **§4 results:** 35 of Altshuller's 40 IPs appear at least once; "an overall uneven diffusion of TRIZ
  IPs". Probability that the IP distribution across contradiction classes is random = 5.3%, i.e. just
  above the 0.05 threshold.
- **§5.1:** "The outcomes do not allow the authors to infer the overall deterministic nature of suitable
  IPs in light of the conflicting elements ... At the same time, stating the complete randomness of IPs'
  choice would be likewise misleading"; domains "have some favourite IPs" (e.g. Segmentation, Parameter
  changes for human-related problems; Dynamics for mechanical).
- **§6:** "the doubts about its development and scientific validation suggest acquiring more knowledge of
  its underlying processes"; IPs "are to be considered as one of the multiple tools TRIZ offers and some
  claim they are not the most effective"; ARIZ and most TRIZ tools prescribe processes "which cannot be
  reconstructed by the sole availability of described problems and solutions".
