# Code Saturation Versus Meaning Saturation: How Many Interviews Are Enough?

## Source structure and scope
Hennink MM, Kaiser BN, Marconi VC. Qualitative Health Research 27(4): 591-608 (DOI
10.1177/1049732316665344; PMC9359070; Europe PMC lists 2017, the PMC page lists online
publication 2016-09-26). Empirical study of 25 in-depth interviews (HIV care retention).
Engagement depth: abstract verbatim; body via a summarising fetch, so body details below are
paraphrase of a tool summary and carry lower confidence.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** "code saturation was reached at nine interviews, whereby the range of thematic
  issues was identified. However, 16 to 24 interviews were needed to reach meaning saturation
  where we developed a richly textured understanding of issues. Thus, code saturation may
  indicate when researchers have 'heard it all,' but meaning saturation is needed to 'understand
  it all.'"
- **Abstract:** results used "to develop parameters that influence saturation", usable to estimate
  sample sizes or "to document in publications the grounds on which saturation was achieved."
- **Body (tool summary, lower confidence):** concrete codes reached meaning saturation by about
  interview 9; conceptual codes needed interviews 16-24; code prevalence did not predict when
  meaning saturation occurred; parameters named include study purpose, population homogeneity,
  sampling strategy, data quality, code type, and codebook complexity/stability; findings framed
  for applied qualitative research.
