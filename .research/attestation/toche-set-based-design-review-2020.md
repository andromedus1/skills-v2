# Set-based design: a review and new directions (Toche, Pellerin & Fortin 2020)

## Source structure and scope
Boris Toche, Robert Pellerin, Clement Fortin; Design Science vol. 6, e18 (2020), doi
10.1017/dsj.2020.16; open access, 41 pages. Sections read: Abstract, §1 Introduction, §2 Set-based
design, §4.1–4.2 (maturity), §5 summaries of contributions (portions on Liker et al. 1996 survey, Toyota
paradox literature, computational confirmatory studies), §6 Conclusions. A literature review of 154
peer-reviewed publications classified by research paradigm and design-process coverage.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** SBD/SBCE "has emerged as an important component of lean product development"; "SBD has a
  relatively low theoretical development"; methods and tools abound but "they rarely rely on a model that
  is in the continuum of a design process model".
- **§1:** studies of LPD in industry show implementation maturity "is low, no more than an introductory
  level and it has not been possible to find a company that coherently combines the LPD enablers"; SBD
  research partly leverages "the claimed efficiency of the design methodology".
- **§2 (point-based vs set-based):** point-based design "focuses on one solution at a time and
  progressively refines it"; in set-based design "engineers may reason and communicate about an acceptable
  range of parametric values instead of the single best value"; "Participants bring sets of possibilities
  to the table and juxtapose them to find an intersection of feasibility rather than successively
  criticizing and modifying a single option".
- **§2 (benefit/cost):** broad discovery "avoids narrowing too quickly on discrete areas of the space and
  thereby losing the potential for innovation and discovery. However, the additional costs incurred by
  supporting multiple alternatives long into the development process could be detrimental." Ward & Sobek
  (2014) use probabilistic calculations "to determine the optimal number of alternatives/prototypes to
  pursue given the probability of failure of a system or subsystem design (product of the probabilities
  for the individual concepts)", focusing broader evaluation where risk is high; review says this "is
  confirmed by Camburn et al. (2017) and Camburn et al. (2015) through empirical research" (as reported).
- **§2 — three SBCE principles (Sobek, Ward & Liker 1999, as reported):** (1) Map the design space:
  define feasible regions; explore trade-offs by designing multiple alternatives; communicate sets of
  possibilities. (2) Integrate by intersection: look for intersections of feasible sets; impose minimum
  constraint; seek conceptual robustness. (3) Establish feasibility before commitment: narrow sets
  gradually while increasing detail; stay within sets once committed; control by managing uncertainty at
  process gates.
- **§2:** morphological chart, Pugh's controlled convergence, design-build-test, etc. "share similarities
  with SBCE"; SBCE specificity is (1) specialty groups analyse their own option sets independently then
  "intersect at integration events" and (2) delaying decisions to learn by prototyping before "narrowing
  the design space by elimination of unfeasible designs".
- **§4.2:** of 154 publications, 40% journal and 60% proceedings; authors argue this "indicates a low
  theoretical development of SBCE".
- **§5 (research paradigm):** "SBCE research is therefore predominantly qualitative and inductive";
  foundational researchers "mainly performed case studies".
- **§5 — Liker et al. 1996 survey (as reported):** 92 Japanese and 119 US automotive suppliers; set-based
  communication more prevalent among Japanese suppliers; the survey's indicator list "is not exhaustive"
  and authors call for better "indicators and metrics for point-based versus set-based design".
- **§5 — confirmatory simulations (as reported):** Shahan & Seepersad (2010) used multi-criteria
  optimization and discrete event simulation, concluding in favour of reduced iteration cycles for
  set-based exchange; simulations "were done on serialized processes", flagged as a limitation.
