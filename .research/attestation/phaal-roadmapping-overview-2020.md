# Roadmapping for strategy and innovation (Phaal, 2020)

## Source structure and scope
Robert Phaal, Centre for Technology Management, Institute for Manufacturing, University of
Cambridge; 9-page overview dated 13 January 2020. Sections: 1 Introduction; 2 Roadmapping in
practice; 3 Implementation success factors; 4 Case study (global packaging company). Read in
full. The author co-developed the S-Plan and T-Plan methods described.

## Anchored details
- **§1:** technology roadmapping originated at Motorola in the 1970s to align technology and
  product development. Galvin's definition: a roadmap "is an extended look at the future of a
  chosen field of inquiry composed from the collective knowledge and imagination of the
  brightest drivers of change in that field". The "most frequently cited benefit" is
  communication across functional and organisational boundaries, building consensus.
- **§1, Fig. 2:** the generic framework is a multi-layered time-based chart that represents both
  "demand" and "supply" side views, "balancing 'market pull' and 'technology push'", linked to
  questions: where do we want to go / where are we now / how can we get there; why / what /
  how / by when. Roadmaps are "simple, adaptable 'strategic lenses'".
- **§1:** a survey of public-domain roadmaps found "more than 2,000 examples"; the term's
  popularisation "has led to a proliferation of so-called 'roadmaps' that do not build on the
  intellectual origins of the approach."
- **§2:** "It is often claimed that the process of developing roadmaps is more important than
  the roadmaps themselves, due to the associated communication and consensus-building
  benefits (although a healthy strategy process should also challenge this consensus, as
  Motorola did with a requirement to include a 'minority report' with their consensus-based
  roadmap)." Workshops are the common ingredient; in S-Plan a large wall chart is used "to
  share perspectives across the full scope of the topic of interest, to create a 'strategic
  landscape'", within which "landmarks" (opportunities, concerns) are identified; small groups
  then explore topics with a common template. "All management tools have strengths and
  weaknesses, and typically several are needed".
- **§3:** a 2003 Dutch Ministry of Economic Affairs study reviewed 78 sector-level roadmapping
  initiatives and derived "good practices and lessons" (e.g. link to strategy, urgency,
  high-level commitment, customise format, "Roadmapping is inherently exploratory", "a spirit of
  openness is important, to encourage new participants and thinking", iterate after the first
  roadmap, monitor outcomes). These are lessons, not measured effects.
- **§4 case:** S-Plan workshops per business unit; priorities compared to the existing R&D
  portfolio; "where gaps were identified budgets were reallocated". Benefits reported
  qualitatively.
