# Patent Landscape Reporting Quality and Predicting Drug Approval (James Andrew Smith, DPhil thesis, 2018)

## Source structure and scope
James Andrew Smith, Kellogg College, University of Oxford; DPhil thesis, Trinity Term 2018, 241
pages. Engagement depth: **partial** — abstract and Chapter 2 ("Evidence of Insufficient
Quality of Reporting in Patent Landscapes in the Life Sciences: A Systematic Review"), §2.4.3
and §2.5-2.5.1 (thesis pp. 25-27) read; drug-approval chapters not read.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** in reviewing the academic patent literature "it was apparent that improvements in
  the quality of available evidence were needed before patent landscapes ... could be useful";
  a systematic review found reporting "insufficient", leading to the RIPL statement.
- **§2.4 / Fig. 2.3 (p. 25):** compliance correlated positively with year (Spearman rs = 0.409,
  p < 0.001); article type had a significant effect; journal type had none.
- **§2.4.3:** 116 potentially relevant articles could not be included for lack of full text; 85%
  of these were from "other" (non-science) journal types.
- **§2.5.1 (p. 26):** without reporting of eligibility criteria, search strategy, databases,
  dates, offices and components searched, software, patent selection process and analysis
  details, "reproducing and validating a patent landscape methodology is, in our opinion, not
  possible. Eight articles (9.9%) reported all of these methodological items ... Just one article
  (1.4%) reported that patent selection was blindly reproduced, and 24% of articles list the
  patents included in the study".
