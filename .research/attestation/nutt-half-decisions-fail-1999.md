# Surprising but true: Half the decisions in organizations fail (Nutt 1999)

## Source structure and scope
Paul C. Nutt. Academy of Management Executive 13(4), 1999, pp. 75–89. Practitioner-facing synthesis of
the author's case studies of 356 decisions by senior managers in medium to large US and Canadian
organizations (public, private, third sector). Data collected by interviewing key participants; tactics
classified; success measured as sustained use (two years) and full use of the adopted decision.
Engagement depth: full article read; Table 4 values read where OCR was legible.

## Anchored details
- **Executive overview:** failures "can be traced to managers who impose solutions, limit the search for
  alternatives, and use power to implement their plans"; managers who "set objectives, carry out an
  unrestricted search for solutions, and get key people to participate are more apt to be successful."
  "Tactics prone to fail were used in two of every three decisions."
- **p.76 (method):** cases from interviews with key participants including the person responsible;
  "results do not vary much between the different types of decisions and organizations."
- **p.78 (process types):** "The less successful imposition process type uncovers a solution early on and
  does not consider any other solution ideas. The more effective discovery process type establishes
  directions and identifies options as separate activities."
- **p.78 (idea tactic):** used in 37% of cases; "prompts managers to focus on a single solution";
  resulting solutions fully used in 42% and sustained in 56%.
- **p.79 (objectives tactic):** used in 30% of cases; "gives everyone involved considerable freedom to
  search for solution options"; 70% sustained use, 58% full use. "Objectives liberate people to search
  widely for solutions."
- **p.80–81:** "Preconceived solutions and limited searches for options are recipes for failure";
  "Decisions with an idea direction seldom go beyond that idea"; benefits of ideas "will be lost if the
  options under consideration are limited to the initial set of ideas."
- **Table 4 (legible values):** "Existing solution" (imposition) used in 223 of 356 decisions with
  sustained-use 55 and full-use 41; single benchmarking 7% (59/59); integrated benchmarking 6% (71
  sustained, 78 full use); searching 12% of cases. Notes: "Decisions that used multiple tactics were not
  included."
- **p.83:** integrated benchmarking infrequent partly because "perceived time pressure ... often creating
  artificial pressure to adopt the first workable idea that is uncovered."
- **Lessons:** "Identify more than one option. The consideration of several competing options improves
  decision results"; recommends integrated benchmarking, cyclical search and design.
