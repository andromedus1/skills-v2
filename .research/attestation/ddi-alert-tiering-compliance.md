# Tiering Drug-Drug Interaction Alerts by Severity Increases Compliance Rates

## Source structure and scope
Marilyn D Paterno, Saverio M Maviglia, Paul N Gorman, Diane L Seger, Eileen Yoshida, Andrew C Seger, and co-authors
(Partners HealthCare System and others). J Am Med Inform Assoc. 2009 Jan-Feb;16(1):40-46, doi 10.1197/jamia.M2808
(as read on PMC). A retrospective observational "natural experiment": one year (Feb 2004 to Feb 2005) of inpatient
drug-drug interaction (DDI) alert logs at two academic hospitals sharing one DDI knowledge base; one presented all
alerts interruptively (non-tiered), the other tiered them by severity. Only the first instance per patient, drug
pair, and hospitalization was analyzed (71,350 alerts).

## Anchored details
- **Background (the tiering design):** "only the most serious DDIs would require a response by the clinician, with
  less serious ones presented in a non-interruptive fashion, i.e., as information only." Level 1: hard stop; Level 2:
  interruptive, override needs a reason; Level 3: "alerts are presented as information only, and require no action of
  any kind from the clinician", intended "to reduce “noise” from too many alerts".
- **Abstract, Results:**

  > Compliance with DDI alerts was significantly higher at the site with tiered DDI alerts compared to the non-tiered site (29% vs. 10%, p < 0.001). At the tiered site, 100% of the most severe alerts were accepted, vs. only 34% at the non-tiered site

- **Results, Table 2:** Level 3 (least serious) alerts were 71.4% of all alerts; Level 1 0.2%; Level 2 28.4%.
- **Results, Table 3:** Level 2 compliance 11% (1164/10531) non-tiered vs 29% (2782/9731) tiered; Level 3 compliance at
  the non-tiered site 9% (2702/28885). "Level 3 response rates between the sites cannot be directly compared, because
  by design at the tiered site, no response was collected for Level 3 alerts." Level 1 compliance at the tiered site
  was 100% "by design since this was a “hard stop”".
- **Results (by drug pair):** e.g., heparin-enoxaparin "at the non-tiered site, 22% of 1037 alerts were accepted,
  while the rate was 70% of 1091 alerts at the tiered site."
- **Discussion:** "how alerts are prioritized and presented to the user may be as important as which alerts to
  deliver." "Interrupting clinicians only for more serious interactions may make them more receptive to the alerts".
  "Clinicians did not object to these hard stops at the tiered site, and they occur very infrequently". "An open
  question which deserves additional evaluation is what the “ideal” acceptance rate is."
- **Limitations:** observational, two hospitals in one delivery system; no adverse-event data; compliance at the
  non-tiered site partly inferred from free-text override reasons (about 1000 Level 2 alerts reclassified).
