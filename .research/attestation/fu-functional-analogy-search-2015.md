# Design-by-analogy: experimental evaluation of a functional analogy search methodology for concept generation improvement (Fu, Murphy, Yang, Otto, Jensen & Wood)

## Source structure and scope
Katherine Fu, Jeremy Murphy, Maria Yang, Kevin Otto, Dan Jensen, Kristin Wood. Research in Engineering
Design (Springer; received Nov 2013, accepted Nov 2014). 19-page author copy. Read: abstract, §1, §3
method, §4 results, §5 discussion (start).

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** experimentally tests "a new method for extracting functional analogies from general data sources,
  such as patent databases"; "significantly improved results on the novelty of solutions generated and no
  significant change in the total quantity of solutions generated"; uses "a novel functional vector space
  representation to quantify the functional similarity between represented design problems and ... patent
  descriptions".
- **§1 mechanism:** builds "a controlled and converged vocabulary of functions extracted from a patent
  database, building on the hierarchical structure of a functional basis" (Pahl & Beitz lineage; Stone & Wood;
  Hirtz et al.); this "provides a natural abstraction mechanism for analogies"; patents indexed by function;
  queries rank patents by relevance "to the functional description, or alternative functional descriptions, of a
  given design problem." Aim: introduce "nonobvious analogies".
- **§3 design:** 68 senior mechanical-engineering students, 4 groups of 17; three-phase ideation (10 min,
  stimulus phase, 10 min); analogy groups saw function-retrieved patents at three functionality levels
  (1 subfunction, 2 subfunctions, all functions); the control group read "an unrelated article" as a
  distracter. Problem: device to collect energy from human motion (same problem as Chan et al. 2011 and
  Fu et al. 2013).
- **§4.1:** no significant difference in quantity of ideas between groups (control mean 15.12; All Functions
  13.76, p = 0.174).
- **§4.2:** only the "All Functions" group had significantly higher average novelty than control (95% level);
  1-Function and 2-Functions groups did not differ from control; "this effect appears to be dependent on the
  functionality level of the analogy." Example outputs: a floating bridge harvesting energy from walkers
  (from a wave-motion patent); a wireless-power concept where "feasibility issues such as signal attenuation
  limit the practicality".
- **§3.4 / §4:** quality of concepts "was not explicitly evaluated" (authors cite prior correlation of quality
  with quantity and novelty).
- **§5:** null effect on quantity "may indicate that the additional cognitive load required to process the
  analogical stimuli, abstract, and transfer the analogical content, could be canceling out any positive effects".
