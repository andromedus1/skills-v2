# Strategic Foresight in multinational enterprises - a case study on the Deutsche Telekom Laboratories (Rohrbeck, Arnold, Heuer, 2007)

## Source structure and scope
René Rohrbeck, Heinrich M. Arnold, Jörg Heuer (Deutsche Telekom Laboratories). ISPIM-Asia
2007 conference paper, MPRA Paper No. 5700 (PDF fetched from mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de). Defines
a framework of strategic foresight actors, methods and systems from literature and illustrates
it with one Deutsche Telekom project. Engagement depth: full text extracted; introduction,
definitions (§2), methods (§5, Fig. 5) and further-research section read.

## Anchored details
- **§1:** "To discover threats in time, companies have to use weak signals in their periphery";
  illustrated with a Day and Schoemaker (2005, "Scanning the periphery", Harvard Business
  Review) example of two breweries, one of which reacted to the light-beer shift two years late
  (cited through this source).
- **§2:** forecasting (1970s) used past data — trend extrapolation, S-curves, patent and
  publication analysis; foresight broadened to "methods that enable networking for information
  gathering, assessment and interpretation"; strategic foresight uses weak signals from science
  and technology and from political, socio-cultural and competitive environments.
  "Technology Intelligence deals with the identification, assessment and usage of weak signals
  and information about emerging technologies and technological discontinuities."
- **§4:** scanning reports at the company are disseminated with "a strong information-push
  character: readers cannot state what information they require or how comprehensive it
  should be".
- **§5, Fig. 5 method families:** market-oriented (customer diaries, ethnography,
  lead-user/lead-market analysis, competitor analysis, trend reports, surveys); technology-
  oriented (technology scouting, publication analysis, patent analysis, conference analysis,
  S-curves, Delphi, technological competitor analysis, benchmarking, simulations); integrating
  methods (roadmapping, scenario technique, QFD, internal Delphi). Integrating methods are
  "discussed as a powerful means to overcome the barriers between market and technology
  perspective" and between planning levels. Deutsche Telekom "mainly uses an international
  scouting network for Technology Intelligence".
- **Further research:** "no consistent frameworks incorporating actors, methods and systems have
  been proposed"; how to structure foresight to maximise innovative capacity "remains
  unanswered". The paper reports no measurement of coverage or blind-spot reduction.
