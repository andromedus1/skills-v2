# Concept Mapping (Research Methods Knowledge Base)

## Anchored details
- **Definition (quoted):** "concept mapping is a structured process, focused on a topic or
  construct of interest, involving input from one or more participants, that produces an
  interpretable pictorial view (concept map) of their ideas."
- **Six steps:** preparation (select participants, typically 10–20 stakeholders; develop
  the focus); generation (brainstorming, nominal group or focus groups; up to about 200
  statements); structuring (each participant sorts statements into piles of similar
  ideas and names them; rates each statement, e.g. importance 1–5); representation
  (multidimensional scaling places statements as points, closer when sorted together more
  often; cluster analysis partitions the map); interpretation (facilitated labelling);
  utilization.
- **Cluster count:** choosing the number of clusters is described as a judgment call
  rather than a prescriptive criterion.
