# Intuitive Choices in Chess Option-Generation are Task and Expertise Dependent: A Conceptual Replication (Raab et al. 2026)

## Source structure and scope
Markus Raab, Sofie Klenk, Zoltán Medvegy, Ivanna Korobeinikova, Lesia Korobeinikova, Georgiy
Korobeynikov, Elisa Weinand, Tamás Sterbenz. Journal of Expertise 9(1), March 2026, pp. 58–. Conceptual
replication (N=92, mean Elo below 2000) of Medvegy et al. (2022, N=34 players above Elo 2000) on the
Take-the-First heuristic in chess option generation. Engagement depth: abstract, introduction, results on
serial position, and discussion read.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** Medvegy et al. (2022) are reported to have found that experts "generate only a few
  options, and often pick the first one generated." In the replication, "lower-rated players did not show
  a clear quality advantage of the first generated option, suggesting that the Take-the-First heuristic
  becomes reliably effective only at higher levels of expertise." Intuitive choices "were not worse than
  deliberate ones."
- **Introduction, p.59:** whether more options help ("more-is-better") or hurt ("less-is-more") "is
  heavily debated and may depend on the person, the task, and situational factors"; Scheibehenne et al.
  (2010) meta-analysis of 50 studies reported as showing that for the same task more options sometimes
  helped and sometimes hurt.
- **Introduction, p.60 (original study as reported):** mean generated options about 3 (M = 3.11);
  "Players often chose their first generated option as the best move, and move quality decreased from
  the first to the second to the third option"; original limitations: small, selective sample and
  disagreement between grandmaster evaluations and engines.
- **Results:** players below Elo 2000 generated M = 2.2 options and selected their first move as best in
  37.3% of cases; serial position had no significant effect on expert-rated move quality, F(3, 992) =
  1.73, p = .159, although the first option was descriptively higher.
