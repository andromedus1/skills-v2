# The relationship of analogical distance to analogical function and preinventive structure: The case of engineering design (Christensen & Schunn)

## Source structure and scope
Bo T. Christensen (Copenhagen Business School), Christian D. Schunn (Pittsburgh). Memory & Cognition 35(1),
29-38, 2007 (publisher PDF via OSF). Read: abstract, introduction, method, results, discussion portions.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** in vivo study of real-world engineering design; analogising "occur[red] frequently, entailing a
  roughly equal amount of within- and between-domain analogies"; "the reference to exemplars (in the form of
  prototypes) significantly reduced the number of between-domain analogies between source and target, as
  compared with using sketches or no external representational systems" — presented as partial support for
  unconscious plagiarism and Ward's path-of-least-resistance model; analogies served three functions:
  identifying problems (mainly within domain), solving problems (mixed) and explaining concepts (mainly between
  domain).
- **Introduction:** local (within-domain) analogies share more superficial similarity, which "may make local
  analogies easier to access"; design techniques that use analogy include Synectics and TRIZ; anecdotes of
  distant transfer (Velcro) abound — "Regardless of whether these anecdotes are true".
- **Method:** a five-member product-development subgroup in medical plastics (10–35 years' experience), seven
  videotaped meetings, ≈9 h of transcript.
- **Results:** 102 analogy segments (≈11.3 per hour); 55% within-domain, 45% between-domain; between-domain
  sources included potato prints, zippers, credit cards, Venetian blinds. Within-domain analogies shared more
  surface features (M 3.82 of 4) than between-domain ones (M 0.52), t(100) = 17.21. Significantly more
  between-domain analogies occurred around ideas and sketches than around prototypes (χ²(2) = 19.26, p < .001).
  Function split: 28% identify problems, 40% solve problems, 32% explain.
