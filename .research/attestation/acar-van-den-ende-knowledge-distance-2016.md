# Knowledge Distance, Cognitive-Search Processes, and Creativity: The Making of Winning Solutions in Science Contests (Acar & van den Ende)

## Source structure and scope
Oguz Ali Acar (King's College London; Erasmus RSM), Jan van den Ende (Erasmus RSM). Psychological Science
27(5) 692–699, 2016; open-access repository copy, 8 pages. Read in full.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** "Prior research has provided conflicting arguments and evidence about whether people who are
  outsiders or insiders relative to a knowledge domain are more likely to demonstrate scientific creativity"; in
  "230 solutions generated in a science contest platform", "distance was positively associated with creativity
  when problem solvers engaged in a focused search (i.e., low cognitive-search variation) and exerted a high level
  of cognitive effort"; insiders were more creative "when they drew on a wide variety of different knowledge
  elements for recombination (i.e., high cognitive-search variation) and exerted substantial cognitive effort."
- **Introduction:** insiders "know the territory"; outsiders may bring fresh perspectives (citing Jeppesen &
  Lakhani 2010); domain knowledge associated with inflexibility, e.g. the Einstellung effect, "when the first idea
  that comes to mind, triggered by previous experience with similar situations, prevents alternatives being
  considered" (Bilalić et al., as quoted).
- **Method:** InnoCentive solvers surveyed; knowledge distance = self-report 1–7 scale from Jeppesen & Lakhani
  (2010) (1 = inside my field, 7 = outside), validated against experience solving similar problems; outcome =
  winning solution.
- **Table 1 (N = 218):** knowledge distance main effect odds ratio 0.99 [0.76, 1.29], p > .25 (not significant);
  search effort OR 3.45, p = .013; search variation n.s.; three-way interaction distance × effort × variation
  OR 0.48 [0.28, 0.82], p = .008.
- **Results (pp.696–697):** knowledge distance "was negatively related to the likelihood of winning when both search
  variation and search effort were high"; association "negligible when search effort was low"; highest odds for
  distant solvers with high effort and low variation; "For high levels of search effort, variation was negatively
  related to creativity for outsiders, whereas this association was positive for insiders."
- **Discussion:** the creativity measured is closer to "radical" or "big C" creativity; offers a cognitive account
  that "may explain conflicting evidence" on distance and creativity.
- **Limitations:** correlational — "did not allow a precise determination of causality"; independent variables are
  self-reports, open to social-desirability bias.
