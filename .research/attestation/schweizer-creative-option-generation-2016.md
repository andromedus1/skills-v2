# Cognitive and Affective Aspects of Creative Option Generation in Everyday Life Situations (Schweizer et al. 2016)

## Source structure and scope
T. Sophie Schweizer, Katja M. Schmalenberger, Tory A. Eisenlohr-Moul, Andreas Mojzisch, Stefan Kaiser,
Joachim Funke. Frontiers in Psychology 7:1132, published 3 August 2016, doi 10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01132.
N=48 students generated options for 70 short everyday scenarios (8 s mute generation, 8 s verbalization,
then choice). Engagement depth: introduction, methods and results tables read; discussion skimmed.

## Anchored details
- **Introduction, p.2:** distinguishes memory-based views (associative retrieval of options from
  long-term memory) from ideation-based views; situational typology after Kalis et al. (2008):
  constrained situations, unconstrained-familiar (options retrieved from memory), unconstrained-unfamiliar
  (new options must be generated, potentially creative).
- **Introduction, p.3:** reports two conflicting positions — fewer options give higher average quality
  (Johnson & Raab 2003) vs "quantity breeds quality" (Rietzschel et al. 2007) — and that Del Missier et
  al. (2015) found more options may raise best-option quality but lower mean quality.
- **Methods, p.5:** quality rated on originality, feasibility, and divergence (extent the set's options
  differ from each other); inter-rater ICCs 0.42–0.47 ("fair").
- **Results, Table 1 (between-person correlations):** number of options correlated with divergence
  r=0.62 and with maximum originality r=0.53, but with mean originality only 0.11; mean feasibility
  correlated negatively with number of options (r=−0.40), divergence (−0.35) and originality (−0.42).
- **Abstract:** in familiar situations, more negative affective valence was associated with more
  originality and divergence; "a higher option generation fluency was associated with a greater maximal
  originality of options."
