# Does Analogical Distance Affect Performance of Ideation? (Srinivasan, Song, Luo, Subburaj, Elara, Blessing & Wood)

## Source structure and scope
V. Srinivasan, Binyang Song, Jianxi Luo, Karupppasamy Subburaj, Mohan Rajesh Elara, Lucienne Blessing, Kristin Wood.
Journal of Mechanical Design 140(7), article 071101 (published online 2018-05-23 per Crossref). Engagement depth:
abstract only (Crossref metadata record and the SUTD repository landing page). Methods, statistics and effect sizes
were not audited. The same dataset is reported in a 2017 ICED paper by the same authors (attested separately as
srinivasan-stimuli-ideation-iced-2017).

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** "Identifying relevant stimuli that help generate solutions of desired novelty and quality is
  challenging in analogical design"; data from "105 student designers, individually generating 226 concepts of
  spherical rolling robots"; "138 concepts generated with patents as stimuli and the patents used are analyzed";
  "Analogical distance of a patent is measured in terms of knowledge similarity between technology classes
  constituting the patent and design problem domain".
- **Abstract, key observations:** "(a) technology classes in closer than farther distances from the design problem
  are used more frequently to generate concepts, (b) as analogical distance increases the novelty of concepts
  increases, and (c) as analogical distance decreases the quality of concepts increases."
