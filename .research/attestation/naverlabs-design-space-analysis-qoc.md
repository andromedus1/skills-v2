# Design Space Analysis (NAVER LABS Europe past-research page; ex-Rank Xerox EuroPARC project)

## Source structure and scope
Archived project description hosted by NAVER LABS Europe (the successor of the Xerox research centre in
Grenoble) under "history / past research". Undated web page, read in full. Names researchers Allan
MacLean and Victoria Bellotti; collaborators AMODEUS and EuroCODE (Esprit) projects. Sections: intro;
Questions, Options and Criteria; Organisational memory; Researchers; Key reports. Project self-description
— advocacy, no outcome data.

## Anchored details
- **Header:** "Design Space Analysis (DSA) is aimed at helping software designers reason about design
  (individually and in groups) and to produce a representation which can help others understand why the
  resulting design is the way it is." Focus primarily HCI design; "The project has been in existence since
  1988" (AMODEUS, Esprit BRA 7040).
- **Questions, Options and Criteria:** "A key characteristic of the approach is that it focuses on
  producing a design space rather than a single artifact." The representation "provides a succinct
  rationale for the final design by placing it in a broader context which highlights how it might be
  different and why it is the way it is."
- **QOC notation:** "Questions highlight key issues in the design space. Options can be thought of as
  'answers' to Questions. Criteria are the reasons that argue for or against possible Options." Option–
  criterion links are positive (solid line) or negative (dotted). "Options may spawn off consequent
  Questions which allow more detailed aspects of the design to be addressed."
- **Organisational memory:** attempts to preserve records "typically fail either because the overheads
  are too great for producing the records or for retrieving information from them"; video libraries of
  meetings failed because information cannot be found "in a reasonable time and because much of the real
  work in design doesn't happen in meetings anyway".
- **Scope claim:** "We have developed DSA in such a way that it does not make any specific claims about
  what information should be included. Its contribution is in providing a means of structuring the
  information being used." "The information may come from another design method, or simply the prior
  experience of the designer."
- **Key reports listed:** MacLean, Young, Bellotti & Moran (1991) "Questions, Options, and Criteria:
  Elements of Design Space Analysis", Human-Computer Interaction 6(3&4), pp. 201–250; McKerlie & MacLean
  (1994) "Reasoning with Design Rationale: Practical Experience with Design Space Analysis", Design
  Studies 15, pp. 214–226; MacLean, Bellotti & Shum (1993) "Developing the Design Space with Design Space
  Analysis".
