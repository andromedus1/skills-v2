# Wie man trotz Unerfahrenheit gute Entscheidungen treffen kann (Universität Bayreuth press release 162/2016)

## Source structure and scope
University of Bayreuth press release no. 162/2016, 8 December 2016, German, 5 pages, read in full.
Institutional report of Johannes Siebert, "Can Novices Create Alternatives of the Same Quality as
Experts?", Decision Analysis (2016), doi 10.1287/deca.2016.0339. Numbers and findings are as reported by
the press office, not read off the paper. Translations below are paraphrases from German.

## Anchored details
- **p.1:** 236 participants included in analysis (123 male, 113 female): bachelor/master students,
  doctoral students and postdocs at four German universities and an international summer school in
  Sweden, mostly in economics and adjacent fields.
- **p.2 (task):** participants proposed several alternative titles for a decision-theory publication whose
  original title they did not know, improving their alternatives in a multi-stage procedure; one group
  ("Kontrollgruppe" in the release) was explicitly challenged to reflect on objectives while developing
  alternatives. Each title scored on content, language and interest; the whole set of titles proposed by a
  participant was also rated.
- **p.2 (set quality):** "Ein Set aus wenigen, aber klar unterscheidbaren Alternativen schnitt dabei besser
  ab als ein Set aus zahlreichen, aber einander ähnlichen Alternativen" — a set of few but clearly
  distinguishable alternatives scored better than a set of many but mutually similar alternatives.
- **p.3:** reflecting on objectives raised the quality of alternatives regardless of prior experience;
  experienced decision makers generally considered better alternatives than inexperienced ones; prompting
  objectives increased the number of objectives considered, and the more objectives, the better the
  quality of alternatives considered. The release states the 2015 Siebert & Keeney finding is "eindeutig
  bestätigt" (clearly confirmed).
