# Creating More and Better Alternatives for Decisions Using Objectives (Siebert & Keeney 2015)

## Source structure and scope
Johannes Siebert and Ralph L. Keeney, Operations Research 63(5), 1144–1158, October 2015, doi
10.1287/opre.2015.1411. Engagement depth: abstract and bibliographic record (RePEc/IDEAS) only; the
publisher full text was behind a bot-challenge page for this fetch tool and was not read. Study designs,
samples and effect sizes are therefore not audited here (see the separate press-release attestation
`siebert-keeney-press-2015` for the institution's reported numbers).

## Anchored details
- **Abstract, sentence 2:** "based on five empirical studies of important personally relevant
  decisions, examines the ability of decision makers to create alternatives for their important decisions
  and the effectiveness of different stimuli for improving this ability."
- **Abstract, sentence 3:** "For decisions for which the full set of potentially desirable alternatives is
  not readily apparent, our first study indicates that decision makers identify less than half of their
  alternatives and that the average quality of the overlooked alternatives is the same as those
  identified."
- **Abstract, sentence 4:** "Four other studies provide insight about how to use objectives to stimulate
  the alternative-creation process of decision makers and confirm with high significance that such use
  enhances both the number and quality of created alternatives."
- **RePEc reference list (works cited by the paper):** Keeney 2012 "Value-Focused Brainstorming"
  (Decision Analysis 9(4)); Bond, Carlson & Keeney 2010 "Improving the Generation of Decision Objectives"
  (Decision Analysis 7(3)); Gettys, Pliske, Manning & Casey 1987 "An evaluation of human act generation
  performance" (OBHDP 39(1)).
