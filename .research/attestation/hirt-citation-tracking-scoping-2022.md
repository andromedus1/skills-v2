# Citation tracking for systematic literature searching: a scoping review

## Source structure and scope
Hirt J, Nordhausen T, Appenzeller-Herzog C, Ewald H. medRxiv preprint posted 2022-09-29 (DOI
10.1101/2022.09.29.22280494, printed in the PDF footer); published version Research Synthesis
Methods 2023; 14: 563-579 (DOI 10.1002/jrsm.1635, per Europe PMC record). Scoping review of 47
methodological studies (1985-2021) on citation tracking (CT) in health-related searching.
Engagement depth: full preprint text (introduction, methods, results, discussion).

## Anchored details
- **Definitions (Introduction):** backward CT "identifies references that were cited by a seed
  reference"; forward CT "identifies citing references"; indirect CT covers co-cited and
  co-citing references; "CT iterations" re-use newly retrieved relevant references as new seeds.
- **Own search (Results):** 24 of 50 included reports (48%) came from supplementary searching
  (one web, 14 backward CT, six forward CT, three expert contact); "Further CT iterations did not
  yield any additional records"; three iterations were run "until no further eligible
  references or pertinent reviews could be identified".
- **Benefit:** "Forty-three of the 45 studies that assessed the benefit of CT found added value";
  the authors caution these numbers "should be interpreted with caution" because of
  heterogeneity and because "the number 2 versus 43 in fact underestimates the ratio of no added
  value versus added value of CT due to publication bias."
- **Context:** 40% of authors reporting added value tied it to specific contexts - designs
  (observational, prognostic, diagnostic) or topics regarded as "complex, broad, fringe, or
  difficult-to-locate".
- **Methods compared:** 70% assessed backward and 62% forward CT; in three comparisons backward
  retrieved more, in two forward retrieved more. Most studies definitely (26%) or probably (62%)
  "omitted CT iterations and performed only one round of CT".
- **No-added-value cases:** Westphal et al. found backward/forward CT via SCI/SSCI led to no
  further included primary studies; Wright et al.'s forward CT on 40 included papers "found only
  one eligible paper that was not previously identified by database searching".
- **Discussion:** CT "may be less beneficial" with clearly defined PICO clinical interventions
  than with "hard-to-search-for topics"; "it is currently challenging for reviewers to recognize
  situations when database searches are not sufficiently exhaustive".
