# The Impact of Analogies on Creative Concept Generation: Lessons From an In Vivo Study in Engineering Design (Chan & Schunn)

## Source structure and scope
Joel Chan, Christian Schunn (Pittsburgh LRDC). Cognitive Science (2014) 1–30, DOI 10.1111/cogs.12127
(author-hosted PDF via OSF). Read: abstract, introduction, Study 1 method and discussion (§2), Study 2 overview
(§3), general discussion caveats.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** tested "the hypothesis that far analogies lead directly to very novel concepts via large steps in
  conceptual spaces (jumps)" in a professional design team's brainstorming; "Surprisingly, we found that concepts
  were more similar to their preceding concepts after far analogy use compared to baseline"; "Yet far analogies
  increased the team's concept generation rate"; results "challenge the view that far analogies primarily lead
  to novel concepts via jumps" and suggest "iterative, deep exploration within a functional space".
- **§2.2:** two transcribed meetings segmented into 4,594 lines; 144 analogies coded (79 and 65).
- **§2.4:** "no support for the hypothesis that far analogies would lead to more jumps than hops"; far analogy
  use "was associated with conceptual moves that were more incremental than concept generation using other
  thought processes"; robust across time windows and measures.
- **§3 framing:** suppression of functional distance "might be seen as evidence of fixation"; Study 2 tested
  whether far analogies reduced fluency — they increased concept generation rate instead.
- **Caveats (p.22):** "the sample size ... was essentially N = 1, given that only one team was studied"; the team
  worked many subproblems; the data cannot relate patterns to final creative outcomes, "thus, only descriptive
  (not prescriptive) inferences are supported".
