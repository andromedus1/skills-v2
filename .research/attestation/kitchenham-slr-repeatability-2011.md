# Repeatability of systematic literature reviews (Kitchenham, Brereton, Li, Budgen, Burn)

## Source structure and scope
Barbara Kitchenham, Pearl Brereton (Keele), Zhi Li (Guangxi Normal University), David Budgen,
Andrew Burn (Durham). 10-page paper; page footers read "Proceedings of EASE 2011" (the mirror's
file name says 2010). Read in full: abstract, §1-2 method, §3 results, §4 discussion and
limitations, §5 conclusion. Compared the study-identification stage of two independent SLRs of
unit testing (by two novice research assistants, restricted to ACM and IEEE libraries 1986-2005)
with each other and with a published expert literature review (Juristo et al., "JMV").

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** "The two SLRs selected very different papers with only six papers out of 32 in
  common, and both differed substantially from a published secondary study of unit testing
  papers finding only three of 21 papers. Of the 29 additional papers found by the novice
  researchers, only 10 were considered relevant. The 10 additional relevant papers would have had
  an impact on the results of the published study by adding three new categories to the
  framework and adding papers to three, otherwise empty, cells."
- **§3 explanations for low overlap:** the novices' search strings did not include "regression
  testing" (their only overlap with JMV was three regression-testing papers); the expert review
  may have silently rejected poor-quality papers or concentrated on prominent ones.
- **§4.1:** after applying criteria, 10 papers not in JMV remained and the authors "could not find
  any reason related to the quality or importance of the papers to explain why at least some of
  the 10 papers should have been omitted"; this "confirm[s] potential problems with the rigour
  and completeness of literature reviews that do not use a well-specified SLR process."
- **§4.2:** 8 of the 10 additional papers would have changed JMV results (three new framework
  classes; three empty classes populated): "missing papers can have a substantial impact on
  conclusions." Contrasts with an earlier case where a restricted manual search and a broad
  automated search gave little change — possibly because that was a mapping study whose
  publication trends changed little.
- **§4.3 Limitations:** single case; RAs developed their own research questions from a general
  topic, so differences may reflect question differences; JMV did not claim to be an SLR.
- **§5:** same broad topic "will not guarantee repeatability" for novices; repeatability requires
  fully documenting search strings and inclusion/exclusion criteria; next step is a search
  "that can (to a certain extent) be validated for completeness".
