## Source structure and scope
Literature analysis: Google Scholar citation analysis of Nickerson et al. (252 citing
items to Oct 2018) plus keyword search ("taxonomy") of AIS Senior Scholars' Basket, ICIS,
ECIS and DESRIST proceedings — 446 identified, 356 unique; coded for building,
evaluating, and reporting evaluation criteria.

## Anchored details
- **Abstract:** "there is virtually no guidance on how to rigorously evaluate them";
  "only a minority of taxonomy building projects actually evaluate their taxonomies and
  that there is no consistency across the multiplicity of criteria used."
- **§2.1:** objective ending conditions "determine when to stop"; subjective ones "seek to
  ensure high quality while building"; "It is unclear to what extent the ending
  conditions can also be used (or already are used) for the evaluation of completed
  taxonomies."
- **§3 counts and reliability:** of 356 unique articles, 201 develop a taxonomy, 63
  evaluate one, 54 report evaluation criteria. Inter-rater percent agreement 98%
  (development), 97% (evaluation), 92% (criteria); Cohen's kappa / Krippendorff's alpha
  0.89–0.96 for development/evaluation coding, 0.62 for evaluation-criteria coding.
- **§4:** 43 different evaluation criteria across 54 articles (Table 1). Most frequent:
  usefulness 34, comprehensiveness 25, applicability 24, robustness 21, conciseness 21,
  extensibility 21, explanatory 18; collectively exhaustive 7; mutually exclusive 6;
  completeness 7; reliability 4.
- **§4.1 ending conditions in practice:** of 63 evaluating articles, 38 apply Nickerson
  et al.'s method; of these, "16 out of 38 articles explicitly state that they apply the
  ending conditions and do this without giving any further detail"; six give detail; two
  justify fulfilment in context. Some articles exclude conditions: one excludes "all
  objects or a representative sample of objects have been examined" because the sample is
  "neither comprehensive nor representative"; another excludes "at least one object is
  classified under every characteristic" because characteristics were conceptually derived
  and "not yet implemented by real-life objects". Subjective conditions: 4 of 38 merely
  cite them; 20 state all or a subset; 10 elaborate; 10 apply a subset or customise the
  set.
- **§4.2:** "some authors state that the usefulness of taxonomies can only be evaluated by
  observing a taxonomy's application over time"; DSR literature "advocates that a rigorous
  research process should strictly separate building from evaluation".
- **§5 Discussion:** "half of them [criteria] appear only once in the sample"; "the five
  subjective ending conditions of this method are often employed not only to determine the
  taxonomy building but also to evaluate the taxonomy after completion."
- **Guidelines (§5):** G1 scope evaluation (what and for whom); G2 "Justify the selection
  of the objective ending conditions and its degree of fulfillment" — e.g. one article
  "explicitly exclude[s] one of the objective ending conditions (mutually exclusive) and
  justify this with a more readable and less complex taxonomy"; G3 contextualize subjective
  conditions; G4 demonstrate applicability — e.g. "consult researchers working on a similar
  topic but not involved in building the taxonomy"; ask target-group representatives "to
  classify objects … This allows to verify whether all objects can be classified and
  whether all the objects' characteristics are covered"; G5 evaluate usefulness (case
  study, field or controlled experiment with treatment/control); G6 re-evaluate robustness
  over time (repeat object search, e.g. 2017 → 2018 → 2019 re-evaluation).
- **§6 limitations:** "the frequency of taxonomy evaluation criteria employed in articles
  does not yet provide justificatory knowledge"; the guidelines "require extensive
  evaluation itself".
