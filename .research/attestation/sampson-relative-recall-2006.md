# An alternative to the hand searching gold standard: validating methodological search filters using relative recall

## Source structure and scope
Sampson M, Zhang L, Morrison A, Barrowman NJ, Clifford TJ, Platt RW, Klassen TP, Moher D. BMC
Medical Research Methodology 2006; 6: 33 (DOI 10.1186/1471-2288-6-33, PMC1557524). Methods
study using 105 Cochrane reviews. Engagement depth: full text (abstract, background, methods,
limitations paragraph).

## Anchored details
- **Problem:** recall "is difficult to measure except in experimental conditions"; "The real
  world difficulty in determining the recall of a search strategy is in knowing the
  denominator." Gold standards have traditionally been built by hand searching, which has "the
  lowest yield per time unit" of commonly used methods.
- **Definition:** "Relative recall is the proportion that any specific system retrieves of the
  total or pooled relevant documents retrieved by all systems considered to be working as a
  composite". Each database and each non-database strategy (expert contact, reference lists,
  hand searching) "can be considered as an approach to the literature."
- **Results (abstract):** 72% of included primary studies were indexed in MEDLINE; relative
  recall of three filter variants "ranged from .98 to .91"; congruent with previously published
  figures; approach needs "approximately 100 studies" from the included studies of multiple
  comprehensively searched reviews.
- **Limitation:** "relative recall is only as good as the sum of the individual searches. It is
  theoretically possible to search a number of sources and still fail to retrieve an important
  number of relevant items"; "The relative recall of one system may be high, yet the actual
  recall could be close to zero" (attributed to Fricke). Mitigation proposed: determine
  performance across a number of systematic reviews.
